1. Introduction

1.1 What Listo is

Listo is an app for shared lists: shopping, tasks and gift ideas. It's designed for a family or a small group of people close to each other, and it's mostly used on a phone.

When someone checks an item, the others see it checked within a few seconds. Listo is free, with no ads and no tracking.

Accounts are created by the Admin, the person who manages Listo: there's no public sign-up.

1.2 What Listo doesn't do

  • No online version: you install Listo on your own server (a computer that's always on, at home for example). See 3. Installation.
  • In French or English, as each person chooses in “My account” (5.19 Language: French or English); this help is available in both languages too.
  • The Notifications page (the bell on Home) keeps what Listo tells you about. Notifications on your phone or computer are optional: turned off by default on the server, then turned on by each person on their own devices (6. Notifications).
  • No payments: for a group gift, Listo shows who's chipping in, never how much.
  • No data is sent anywhere other than your server, with three exceptions you can turn off: checking for a new version (GitHub, 10.9 Knowing a new version exists), the name of a scanned product (Open Food Facts, operations guide in the repository, in French) and notifications, turned off by default (the device's notification service sees the time, the size and the device, never the text: 6.7 What Apple, Google, Mozilla and Microsoft see). If you connect an AI assistant (Claude, for example), the content of the lists and notes you allow is sent to it when you ask it something (9. AI assistants (MCP)).

1.3 Three types of list

  • “Shopping”: items are sorted by aisle (‘Produce’, ‘Dairy’…), or by store for a trip to several shops (“Multi” store). Listo remembers the usual aisle of each item.
  • “Tasks”: things to do, with a priority and a due date if needed.
  • “Wishes”: gift ideas for one person. The people close to them reserve a wish so the same thing isn't given twice, without that person knowing.

You choose the type when you create the list. A shopping list can become a task list, and the other way around, without losing anything. Becoming a wish list is permanent.

1.4 Vocabulary

  • Item: a line in a shopping list or a task list. Wish: a line in a wish list. They all work the same way.
  • Owner: the person who created the list.
  • Contributor: a person who has the “Can edit” permission on the list.
  • Read only: a person who can only read the list.
  • Visitor: a person without an account who opens a list's public link.
  • Recipient: the person a wish list is for.
  • Note: free text, separate from the lists, yours alone at first; it can be shared like a list (5.12 Notes).
  • Admin: the account that manages accounts, stores and aisles.

2. What you can do

What Listo offers today, by topic. Each point links to the section that explains it.

2.1 Lists shared live

  • A list can be shared with other accounts, each with their own permission: “Can edit” or “Read only” (8.4 Owner: sharing and choosing permissions).
  • Changes reach the other phones within a few seconds. Checked items move to the bottom, crossed out.
  • What's new: Listo shows what the others have added or changed since your last visit (5.16 What's new).
  • Notifications: changes (by others), reservations, sharing, the due-date summary and reminders, on the Notifications page (the bell on Home) and, if you want, on your phone or your computer (6. Notifications).
  • An icon of your choice for each list, and a count of what's left on each card, each group and at the top of each list (5.9 Groups, sorting and counter).

2.2 Shopping: aisles and stores

  • Items sorted by aisle; Listo remembers the usual aisle of each item, suggests names you've already typed and “Often added” items (5.1 Add, edit, check, delete).
  • “Multi” store: a trip to several shops, where each item has its store instead of an aisle (8.2 Owner: creating a list).
  • An aisle is used in every store (“All stores”), or in a single one: each list offers the aisles of its store and those of all stores.
  • Anyone can create a missing store or aisle; the Admin manages them (8.1 Admin).

2.3 Tasks: priority, due date, time, reminder, repeat, assignment

  • Priority (low, normal, high) and due date, turned on list by list (8.3 Owner: editing the list).
  • A time, and a reminder chosen task by task (5.2 Time and reminder).
  • Tasks that repeat: every day, on certain days of the week, every month, every year (5.3 Repeating tasks).
  • Assign a task to a person on the list, and see “Mine” (5.4 Assign a task).
  • Put subtasks under a main task (5.21 Subtasks).
  • Sort by priority or by due date (5.9 Groups, sorting and counter).

2.4 Wishes: reserving and chipping in

  • For yourself, for another account or for someone without an account; link, approximate price, priority and photo (8.5 Owner: wish list).
  • The people close to the recipient reserve a wish, possibly several of them together (chipping in) (5.14 Reserve a wish, chip in).
  • The person the list is for never sees the reservations (8.11 Recipient of a wish list).
  • Through the public link, a visitor without an account can also reserve or chip in (8.10 Public link visitor).

2.5 Quick entry

  • Several items at once, separated by commas or line breaks (5.6 Add several items at once).
  • Quantity read from the name: ‘6 eggs’ or ‘eggs x6’ (5.5 Type the quantity with the name).
  • Scan mode: a photo of a product's barcode gives its name (5.7 Scan a barcode).
  • A photo of an item (5.8 Add a photo).

2.6 Notes

  • Notes in free text, that save themselves, with clickable links, a pin and their own trash (5.12 Notes).
  • Simple formatting in notes: headings, bold, italic, underline, highlight, lists, checkboxes and links, with a bar of buttons above the keyboard (5.12 Notes).
  • A note is shared like a list (“Can edit” or “Read only”), or through a read-only public link (5.13 Sharing a note).
  • A quick note from Home, even offline (5.12 Notes).
  • An AI assistant can read your notes and, if you allow it, create notes or add to them (9.6 Notes).

2.7 Offline, trash, public link

  • Offline: Listo opens and works without a network; changes are sent when the network comes back (5.17 Offline and sync).
  • Trash: a deleted list can be restored for 30 days (8.7 Owner: duplicating, deleting, restoring); so can a note (5.12 Notes). A single “Trash”, in Settings, with two tabs: “Lists” and “Notes” (and “Projects” when you use them: 7.10 Project trash).
  • Public link: show a list to someone without an account, for a length of time you choose, with a password if needed (8.6 Owner: the public link); a note too (5.13 Sharing a note).

2.8 Security and convenience

  • Two-factor authentication, with recovery codes (4.7 Two-factor authentication and recovery codes).
  • Automatic backup every night (3.8 Backups).
  • Light, dark or automatic theme (5.18 Light or dark theme).
  • A Home screen your way: tiles hidden or in another order, or no Home screen at all (5.11 Getting around: Home, Due).
  • Only the features you use: notes can be turned off for you alone, without deleting anything (5.11 Getting around: Home, Due).
  • Install it on your phone's home screen, like an app (4.5 Installing Listo on your phone).
  • An AI assistant, for example Claude, can read your lists, notes and projects and, if you allow it, write to them (9. AI assistants (MCP)).

3. Installation

For the person who runs the server

3.1 What you need

  • A server: a computer that is always on, running Linux, with Docker and Docker Compose. Note its address on the local network (for example 192.168.1.20): this is the IP_LOCALE used below.
  • An HTTPS reverse proxy, on this server or on another machine: Nginx Proxy Manager, Caddy, Traefik, nginx, Apache or another one, it doesn't matter which. It needs a subdomain that points to it (for example listo.exemple.fr) and a valid HTTPS certificate for that name (Let's Encrypt, for example). What it must do: 3.4 What the proxy must do.
  • A password manager, to keep Listo's key in (3.7 Keep the secret key safe).

Listo is used over HTTPS: without HTTPS, it refuses to start in production, because signing in, offline mode and installing on the phone all need it.

3.2 The folder and docker-compose.yml

A single file is enough, no source code:

mkdir -p /opt/listo && cd /opt/listo
# copy the repository's docker-compose.yml file here, then open it to edit it

In docker-compose.yml (commented line by line), replace two values:

  1. LISTO_PUBLIC_URL=https://listo.exemple.fr: the exact address you will type in the browser, without a / at the end. A different address makes every connection get refused (“unknown origin”).
  2. IP_LOCALE: the server's address on the local network (192.168.1.20:13300:13300). Never 0.0.0.0: the port must only be open on the local network, and only the proxy should use it.

The data/ folder (database, backups, photos, log) and the secrets/ folder (encryption key) are created automatically on first start, next to the file. They must never be copied anywhere except into your backups.

3.3 Settings (variables)

All the variables are in docker-compose.yml. None of them contains a password or a key.

The environment: part of the file, as shipped (the commented-out lines, starting with #, are optional: remove the # to use them):

    environment:
      - NODE_ENV=production
      - PORT=13300
      - TZ=Europe/Paris   # backup around 3 AM, due-date summary at 8 AM
      - LISTO_PUBLIC_URL=https://listo.exemple.fr
      - LISTO_DB_PATH=/data/listo.db
      - LISTO_KEY_PATH=/secrets/secret.key
      - LISTO_SESSION_DAYS=30
      # Default language (fr or en) and currency for wishes (EUR, USD, CHF…)
      # - LISTO_LANG=fr
      # - LISTO_CURRENCY=EUR
      # - LISTO_UPDATE_CHECK=off
      # - LISTO_PRODUCT_LOOKUP=off
      # - LISTO_PRODUCT_CONTACT=https://listo.exemple.fr/contact
      # LISTO_MCP: access for AI assistants, off by default (“AI assistants (MCP)” chapter)
      # Optional: only accept these sites (otherwise, any AI assistant)
      # - LISTO_MCP_SITES=claude.ai,claude.com
      # Push notifications: OFF by default. Encrypted content: Apple,
      # Google, Mozilla or Microsoft only see the time, the size and the
      # device. Keys in secrets/vapid.json: back it up along with secret.key.
      # - LISTO_PUSH=on
      # Sign-in with an identity provider (Authelia, Authentik, Pocket ID…):
      # OFF by default (Installation chapter). Client secret in
      # secrets/oidc-client-secret.
      # - LISTO_OIDC=on
      # - LISTO_OIDC_ISSUER=https://auth.exemple.fr
      # - LISTO_OIDC_CLIENT_ID=listo
      # - LISTO_OIDC_NAME=Authelia
      # Password sign-in: on by default; off = identity provider only
      # - LISTO_LOCAL_LOGIN=off

NODE_ENV

Production value: production

What it does: Production mode: secure cookies, HTTPS required.

PORT

Production value: 13300

What it does: Port Listo listens on inside the container (the same as in ports:).

TZ

Production value: Europe/Paris

What it does: Time zone: time of the nightly backup (3 AM), of the due-date summary (8 AM), and dates in the log. Set it: without it, the server counts in universal time (one or two hours earlier than in Paris).

LISTO_PUBLIC_URL

Production value: https://listo.exemple.fr

What it does: Exact public address, which must start with https:// in production (otherwise Listo refuses to start and explains why in docker logs listo).

LISTO_DB_PATH

Production value: /data/listo.db

What it does: Database (data/ folder).

LISTO_KEY_PATH

Production value: /secrets/secret.key

What it does: Encryption key, created on first start (secrets/ folder).

LISTO_SESSION_DAYS

Production value: 30

What it does: How long a session lasts without signing in again, in days.

LISTO_LANG

Production value: not set: fr

What it does: Default language, fr or en: the one used by an account set to “Automatic” when the browser doesn't give a known language, and for that account's notifications. Any other value: Listo doesn't start and explains why in docker logs.

LISTO_CURRENCY

Production value: not set: EUR

What it does: Currency for wish prices, as a three-letter code (EUR, USD, CHF, GBP…), with two decimals. Only the display changes: prices already entered are not converted. Any other value: Listo doesn't start and explains why in docker logs.

LISTO_COMMIT

Production value: set by the image

What it does: Installed version, shown in Settings › “About”. Don't fill it in.

LISTO_UPDATE_CHECK

Production value: not set: on

What it does: off turns off the update check on GitHub (10.9 Knowing a new version exists).

LISTO_PRODUCT_LOOKUP

Production value: not set: on

What it does: off turns off scan mode: button hidden, no more requests to Open Food Facts (operations guide (in French), in the repository).

LISTO_PRODUCT_CONTACT

Production value: not set

What it does: Optional: contact email address or web address sent to Open Food Facts (operations guide (in French), in the repository).

LISTO_MCP

Production value: not set: off

What it does: on turns on access for AI assistants, for example Claude (9.15 Turn on access on the server). Any other value: off.

LISTO_MCP_SITES

Production value: not set: all AI assistants

What it does: Optional: websites of the AI assistants that are accepted, separated by commas (for example claude.ai,claude.com), on top of apps on the computer (localhost). Not set: any AI assistant that follows the standard can ask for access, always after your approval (9.15 Turn on access on the server). A badly written value (https://, path, port, IP address, *): Listo doesn't start and explains why in docker logs.

LISTO_PUSH

Production value: not set: off

What it does: on turns on notifications (6.8 Turning the feature on on the server); keys created in secrets/vapid.json. Any other value: off.

LISTO_OIDC

Production value: not set: off

What it does: on turns on sign-in with an OpenID Connect identity provider (3.10 Sign-in with an identity provider (optional)); also requires LISTO_OIDC_ISSUER and the secrets/oidc-client-secret file. Any other value: off.

LISTO_OIDC_ISSUER

Production value: not set

What it does: Address of the identity provider (its ‘issuer’), starting with https:// (for example https://auth.exemple.fr).

LISTO_OIDC_CLIENT_ID

Production value: not set: listo

What it does: Listo's ID at the identity provider (client_id).

LISTO_OIDC_NAME

Production value: not set: SSO

What it does: Provider name shown on screen, for example Authelia: the “Sign in with …” button, “My account”, Administration (30 characters at most).

LISTO_LOCAL_LOGIN

Production value: not set: on

What it does: off turns off sign-in with username and password: you can only get in through the identity provider (requires LISTO_OIDC=on), and sessions opened with a password are closed (3.10 Sign-in with an identity provider (optional)).

3.4 What the proxy must do

Any reverse proxy will do, as long as it does the following. These requirements come from Listo's code; how to set them depends on the product (see its documentation, or the Nginx Proxy Manager example: 3.5 Example with Nginx Proxy Manager).

  1. Terminate HTTPS with a valid certificate for the subdomain, and redirect http:// to https://. HTTPS is required: in production, Listo refuses to start if LISTO_PUBLIC_URL doesn't start with https://, its sign-in cookies are ‘Secure’ (the browser only sends them over HTTPS), and installing on the Home Screen as well as offline mode need HTTPS.
  2. Forward the whole subdomain, at its root and without changing paths, to http://IP_LOCALE:13300 (plain HTTP on the local network). Listo can't be installed in a subfolder of another site (an address ending in …/listo doesn't work). The address typed in the browser must be exactly LISTO_PUBLIC_URL: every change is refused (“unknown origin”) if the Origin header sent by the browser doesn't match it, so the proxy must not rewrite this header.
  3. Let real-time updates through: /api/events is an HTTP connection that stays open (Server-Sent Events, type text/event-stream), not a WebSocket. The proxy must neither hold back nor cache its responses (Listo sends the X-Accel-Buffering: no header, which nginx understands), and its read timeout must be longer than 25 seconds: Listo sends a signal every 25 seconds on this connection. Otherwise, changes arrive late or the connection keeps getting cut.
  4. Accept uploads of at least 2 MB: an item photo is 2 MB at most (Listo refuses anything bigger). Many proxies accept less by default (nginx: 1 MB); allow a little margin, for example 5 MB.
  5. Pass on the visitors' real IP address: Listo trusts a single proxy and reads the last address in the X-Forwarded-For header, which the proxy must fill in with the person's address. It's used to block repeated password attempts. With two proxies one behind the other (an online service in front of your proxy, for example), Listo would see the address of the first one, and a block would hit everyone.
  6. Pass on the other request headers unchanged: cookies, Authorization (access for AI assistants, 9.15 Turn on access on the server) and Sec-Fetch-Site.
  7. Add the Strict-Transport-Security (HSTS) header, if the proxy allows it: Listo doesn't send it itself.

Restrict access to Listo's port

Listo believes the IP address the proxy passes on: someone who reached port 13300 directly could make one up and get around the blocking of password attempts. So this port must only be reachable from the proxy's machine, with a firewall rule on the server or on the network (internet box, router).

  • Example with ufw, if the proxy is at 192.168.1.10: sudo ufw allow from 192.168.1.10 to any port 13300 proto tcp. Careful: Docker opens the ports it publishes by going around ufw; you need a Docker-specific setting (the DOCKER-USER chain) or a firewall upstream.
  • Check: from another computer on the network, curl http://IP_LOCALE:13300/healthz must fail; from the proxy's machine, it must answer {"status":"ok"}.
  • The proxy's access log records the addresses of public links that were opened (/p/…). To cut off a link that may have been passed around, stop it from Listo.

3.5 Example with Nginx Proxy Manager

A step-by-step example, with Nginx Proxy Manager (it can be on another machine). In Nginx Proxy Manager, ‘Hosts’, ‘Proxy Hosts’, ‘Add Proxy Host’:

  • Details: ‘Domain Names’ = your subdomain; ‘Scheme’ = http; ‘Forward Hostname / IP’ = IP_LOCALE; ‘Forward Port’ = 13300. ‘Websockets Support’: not needed, leave it unchecked (real-time updates go through an ordinary HTTP connection).
  • SSL: request a Let's Encrypt certificate, check ‘Force SSL’, ‘HTTP/2 Support’ and ‘HSTS Enabled’ (this is what adds the HSTS header).
  • Advanced (‘Custom Nginx Configuration’): paste the four lines below. These are nginx directives: with plain nginx, they go in the location block that forwards to Listo.
client_max_body_size 5m;
proxy_buffering off;
proxy_cache off;
proxy_read_timeout 3600s;
  • client_max_body_size 5m;: without it, photo uploads are refused (nginx accepts 1 MB by default; a Listo photo is 2 MB at most).
  • The other three are for real-time updates: without them, changes only reach the other phone after about a minute.
  • Nginx Proxy Manager already fills in the X-Forwarded-For header with the phones' real IP address: nothing to set.
  • Access for AI assistants: its addresses go through the same host, with no extra setting. They must stay reachable from the Internet (9.15 Turn on access on the server).
  • Firewall: only allow Nginx Proxy Manager's address to reach port 13300 (3.4 What the proxy must do).

3.6 First start and setup code

On the server, in /opt/listo:

# downloads the image and starts Listo
docker compose up -d
# shows the setup code
docker logs listo
  1. In docker logs listo, find the setup code, framed by ==== (it looks like ABCD-EFGH-JKLM). Don't share it with anyone.
  2. Open https://listo.exemple.fr: the “Welcome to Listo” screen offers to create the Admin account (4.1 The first Admin).
  3. This code only works once: the screen disappears as soon as an account exists.

3.7 Keep the secret key safe

Right after the first start, display the key and copy it into your password manager (one line of text):

cat /opt/listo/secrets/secret.key

Without it, after a server failure or a restore on another machine, two-factor authentication can no longer be read: everyone has to set it up again (10.8 The secret key was lost). Lists, photos and passwords don't depend on it. It is never copied into the nightly backups, on purpose: keep it separately, once and for all.

With notifications turned on (LISTO_PUSH=on), keep secrets/vapid.json (notification keys) the same way: without it, every device has to subscribe again (6.8 Turning the feature on on the server).

3.8 Backups

  • Every night around 3 AM (TZ time), Listo copies its database into data/backups/ (listo-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM.db), checks that the copy is intact, and keeps the last 14. Each copy, successful or failed, is recorded in the log (backup_success or backup_failure; the log is always in English). A failed copy doesn't stop Listo.
  • On-demand backup, before an update for example (-u node matters: the copy must belong to Listo, like the nightly ones):
docker exec -u node listo node server/backup-now.js

The nightly copy contains neither the photos (data/photos/), nor the log (data/journal.log), nor the secrets/secret.key key (on purpose: a copy of the database alone doesn't let anyone read the two-factor authentication secrets). So back up the whole /opt/listo/data folder with the server's usual backup, and keep the key separately (3.7 Keep the secret key safe). Restoring a backup: operations guide (in French), in the repository.

3.9 Checks once in production

  1. HTTPS: https://listo.exemple.fr opens with the padlock; http://listo.exemple.fr redirects to https://.
  2. Health: https://listo.exemple.fr/healthz answers {"status":"ok"}.
  3. Security headers: run the address through a tool like securityheaders.com. You should see Content-Security-Policy, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, X-Frame-Options, Permissions-Policy, Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy, Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy, X-Robots-Tag (sent by Listo) and Strict-Transport-Security (added by the proxy, see 3.4 What the proxy must do), and no X-Powered-By.
  4. A backup: the next morning, ls -l /opt/listo/data/backups shows a copy from the night, and docker logs listo | grep backup shows the backup_success line.
  5. A test restore, once, with the nightly copy: operations guide (in French), in the repository.
  6. The port is closed: see 3.4 What the proxy must do.
  7. The phone: installing on the Home Screen (4.5 Installing Listo on your phone), then the ‘Vérifier sur les téléphones’ checklist (in French) in docs/developpement.md.

3.10 Sign-in with an identity provider (optional)

With an identity provider at home (Authelia, Authentik or Pocket ID, for example: any provider that follows the OpenID Connect standard), everyone can sign in to Listo with their account at that provider (the “Sign in with …” button), in addition to the Listo username and password, which still work. Off by default; without it, there's no button and the /oidc/… addresses answer 404.

  1. At the provider, create a client (sometimes called an ‘application’) for Listo: confidential (with a client secret), redirect address https://listo.exemple.fr/oidc/retour (Listo's address followed by /oidc/retour), scopes openid and profile, PKCE with S256, client secret sent in the header (client_secret_basic). Write down its ID (client_id) and its plain-text client secret.
  2. Put the plain-text secret, alone on one line, in secrets/oidc-client-secret (next to secret.key), then run chmod 600 secrets/oidc-client-secret. Never in docker-compose.yml or in a variable. Keep a copy of it with the secret key (3.7 Keep the secret key safe).
  3. In docker-compose.yml, uncomment LISTO_OIDC=on, LISTO_OIDC_ISSUER with the provider's address (its ‘issuer’), LISTO_OIDC_CLIENT_ID if the ID isn't listo, and LISTO_OIDC_NAME with the name to display (otherwise “SSO”) (3.3 Settings (variables)), then run docker compose up -d. docker logs listo should show the line ‘Sign-in with … enabled’ (startup messages follow LISTO_LANG: with fr, ‘Connexion avec … activée’). If not, the message says what's missing.

What Listo expects from the provider (the standard):

  • its description at the issuer's address followed by /.well-known/openid-configuration, with its addresses on the same site as the issuer;
  • an ID token signed with RS256, PS256 or ES256 (an unsigned token or one signed with HS256 is refused);
  • the person's username in preferred_username and their name in name (profile scope);
  • to authorize an AI assistant (9.9 Connect an AI assistant) from a session opened through the provider: ask for sign-in again when Listo asks for it (prompt=login, max_age=0) and give its time (auth_time). Otherwise the confirmation doesn't go through: in that case, sign in with your Listo password to authorize the assistant.

Two-factor authentication: if the token says it was done at the provider (amr contains mfa), Listo doesn't ask for its own code again. Otherwise, a Listo account that turned on Listo's two-factor authentication is asked for it again.

Example with Authelia: the client secret is created with docker run --rm authelia/authelia:latest authelia crypto hash generate pbkdf2 --variant sha512 --random --random.length 72 --random.charset rfc3986 (‘Random Password’: the plain-text secret, for secrets/oidc-client-secret; ‘Digest’: its hash, for Authelia). The client is declared in identity_providers.oidc.clients, then you restart Authelia. With authorization_policy: 'two_factor', Authelia handles two-factor authentication. With Authentik, Pocket ID or another provider: the same settings, in its interface.

identity_providers:
  oidc:
    clients:
      - client_id: 'listo'
        client_name: 'Listo'
        client_secret: '$pbkdf2-sha512$…'   # the hash (Digest)
        public: false
        authorization_policy: 'two_factor'
        require_pkce: true
        pkce_challenge_method: 'S256'
        redirect_uris:
          - 'https://listo.exemple.fr/oidc/retour'
        scopes: ['openid', 'profile']
        response_types: ['code']
        grant_types: ['authorization_code']
        token_endpoint_auth_method: 'client_secret_basic'

The Listo server must reach the provider at its public address (LISTO_OIDC_ISSUER), over HTTPS. The /oidc/connexion and /oidc/retour addresses go through the same host as Listo: nothing to set in the proxy.

To get in ONLY through the provider, add LISTO_LOCAL_LOGIN=off (3.3 Settings (variables)): the sign-in screen only shows “Sign in with …”, password sign-in is refused and sessions opened with a password are closed; in Administration, “Add account” no longer asks for a password (the person gets in through the provider, with the same username). If the provider is down, nobody can get in: remove the line (or set it to on), then run docker compose up -d, to get password sign-in back.

The account at the provider opens the Listo account that has the same username (case doesn't matter): create the accounts there with the Listo usernames. On first sign-in, Listo remembers that account: a different account with the same name, later on, is refused (the Admin can use “Unlink … account”, in Administration › Accounts).

4. Accounts

4.1 The first Admin

The very first time Listo is opened, the “Welcome to Listo” screen asks for:

  • “Username”: 2 to 50 characters (letters with no accents, numbers, . _ -);
  • “Password”: at least 10 characters, then “Confirm password”;
  • “Setup code”: the one shown in the server logs (3.6 First start and setup code).

Then “Create Admin account”. This account then creates all the others. A new installation has no stores or aisles: the Admin creates them in “Administration”, “Shopping” tab (8.1 Admin), or everyone creates them as they go, with “New store…” and “New aisle…”.

4.2 Creating the other accounts

The Admin goes to Settings, “Administration”, “Accounts” tab, “Add account”.

  • “Role”: “User”, “Guest” or “Admin” (4.3 Account roles: Admin, User, Guest);
  • “Allowed features”: what the account can use, ticked according to the role (everything for a user, nothing for a guest) (4.3 Account roles: Admin, User, Guest);
  • “Username”: what the person will type to sign in;
  • “Display name (optional)”: the first name shown to the others (‘Added by Marie’);
  • “Temporary password”: to give to the person; they'll replace it the first time they sign in.

4.3 Account roles: Admin, User, Guest

Each account has a role, chosen by the Admin:

  • “Admin”: everything, Administration included (8.1 Admin);
  • “User”: the family, who use Listo normally;
  • “Guest”: someone who helps you (on a project, for example). A guest is always discreet: only the Admin sees them in the list of people (“Share”, “For whom?”), with “Guest · discreet” under their name; the family doesn't see them, and they see nobody in that list.

In a list, a note or a project shared with a guest, the members see each other, as for everyone (people, assigned tasks and cards, comments, history). A family member who shares a project with a guest therefore sees them in that project, and only there.

A guest never shares what they own: no people, no public link; they see “People” instead of “Share”. The Admin shares with them. They're never the person a wish list is meant for either.

On their account, a guest sees the “Guest” badge (Settings, “My account”) and the “Guest account” box.

Allowed features

On an account's page (Administration, “Accounts” tab, ⋮ button), under “Role”, the Admin ticks what the person can use. The tick allows; the person then turns the feature on in Settings, “Features” (5.11 Getting around: Home, Due). Unticked, the feature disappears from their screens and Listo refuses it; nothing is erased, everything comes back if you tick it again.

  • “Notes” and “Projects”: for a user, ticked at first (lists are always there, without a tick); they turn them on themselves. For a guest, nothing is ticked at first; a ticked feature turns on for them right away, and they can turn it off later.
  • “Can create their own projects” (under “Projects”): without it, neither “New project” nor “Duplicate project”; projects shared with them stay usable.
  • “AI assistants”: without it, the person can't connect an AI assistant (9.9 Connect an AI assistant), and their connections are disconnected right away.
  • Note labels are shared by the household: a guest who has notes sees them.
  • An Admin has access to everything: their page has no ticks.

Changing an account's role

  • Administration, “Accounts” tab, the account's ⋮ button: its page opens (on a computer, to the right of the list). Tap “User”, “Guest” or “Admin”.
  • From “User” to “Guest”: a confirmation says what changes, and what they keep (their allowed and turned-on features don't change: untick what's needed afterwards). What's already shared with the person stays shared; their public links are stopped. Not possible while a wish list is meant for them.
  • From “Guest” to “User”: a confirmation says they'll be visible to the whole family again, and will see all the accounts.
  • A guest doesn't become an Admin directly, nor an Admin a guest: go through “User” first. Nobody changes their own role, and there's always at least one active Admin.

4.4 Signing in for the first time

  1. Open Listo's address (for example https://listo.exemple.fr).
  2. Enter your “Username” and your “Password”, then “Sign in”.
  3. With a temporary password, the “New password” screen asks you to choose a new one (at least 10 characters), then “Save”.
  4. You stay signed in for 30 days on this phone (the person who runs the server can change this).

4.5 Installing Listo on your phone

Open Listo's address and sign in, then:

  • iPhone (Safari): ‘Share’ button (square with an arrow), ‘Add to Home Screen’, then ‘Add’.
  • Android (Chrome): ⋮ menu, ‘Install app’ (or ‘Add to Home screen’).

The Listo icon appears. When you open it from the home screen, Listo shows full screen and also works without a network. On iPhone, the installed app has its own sign-in, separate from Safari: you need to sign in to it once.

4.6 Changing your password

Settings, “My account”, “Password”: “Current password”, “New password” (at least 10 characters), “Confirm password”, then “Save”. Your other devices are then signed out, and so are your AI assistant connections (9.10 See and disconnect an AI assistant).

4.7 Two-factor authentication and recovery codes

Recommended for the Admin. You need an authenticator app on your phone (Aegis, Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator…).

  1. Settings, “My account”, “Two-factor authentication”.
  2. “Step 1 of 2”: scan the QR code with the app (or copy the key shown under “Or enter this key”), enter the “6-digit code” it shows, then “Verify and enable”.
  3. “Step 2 of 2”: Listo shows 10 “Recovery codes”, only once. “Copy codes”, keep them in a password manager, then “I've saved my codes”.

After that, each sign-in asks for your password, then the code (“Two-factor authentication”). Without your phone, “Use a recovery code”: each code works only once.

To turn it off: same screen, “Password” and “Code” (from the app, or a recovery code), then “Disable two-factor authentication”.

4.8 Forgotten password, lost phone

  • Forgotten password: ask the Admin. In “Administration”, “Accounts”, the ⋮ button of your account, “Reset password”: they give you a temporary password, to change the next time you sign in. Nothing is sent by email.
  • Lost the phone you use for two-factor authentication, and no recovery codes left: the Admin chooses “Disable two-factor authentication” on your account; you can turn it on again afterwards.
  • Too many attempts: 5 wrong passwords block signing in for 15 minutes.
  • The Admin has lost their own password: see 4.9 Lost Admin account.

4.9 Lost Admin account

For the person who runs the server

If there's another Admin, they use “Reset password” (8.1 Admin). Otherwise, on the server (replace identifiant with the account's username):

docker exec -u node listo node server/admin-secours.js identifiant

A temporary password is shown once: sign in with it, then choose a new one. If two-factor authentication can't be used either, add --remove-2fa at the end of the command. The account's AI assistant connections are cut off (9.10 See and disconnect an AI assistant). What the Admin can and can't do: 8.1 Admin.

4.10 Signing in with an identity provider

If the server offers it, the sign-in screen also shows “Sign in with …”, followed by the name chosen by the Admin (Authelia, Authentik, Pocket ID…; “SSO” if they didn't choose one). Tap it and sign in with that provider: you come back to Listo, signed in. Your account with the provider opens the Listo account that has the same username. Your Listo username and password still work. If the Admin had given you a temporary password, it's erased the first time you sign in through the provider.

When you're signed in through the provider, “My account” says so (“Signed in with …”): your password and two-factor authentication are changed with the provider, not in Listo. Authorizing an AI assistant is then also confirmed with the provider (9.9 Connect an AI assistant).

No Listo account yet: “Your account request has been sent to the Admin”. The Admin sees it in Settings, “Administration” (badge), “Accounts” tab, “Account request” section: “Accept” (they can correct the username and name, and choose the role), or “Decline”. Once it's accepted, sign in again through the provider. A declined request can't be made again for 30 days.

5. Everyday use

The everyday actions, for anyone who can edit the list (the owner or “Can edit”). With “Read only”, you can read, sort and search.

5.1 Add, edit, check, delete

  • Add: type the name in the field at the bottom of the list, then tap “+”. Suggestions appear while you type. In a shopping list, for a new item, Listo asks for its aisle, then remembers it.
  • “Often added” (shopping lists): tap the field without typing anything, or tap “+” right away (without the keyboard), to see the items added most often at this store over the last two months, except those already on the list to buy. One tap adds the item (if it is in “In the cart”, it comes back to the list); the panel stays open so you can add more. The “Close” cross, or a tap on the list, closes it; as soon as you type, the usual suggestions come back. An item you no longer buy: swipe its row to the left, then “Remove” (on a computer, the crossed-out eye at the end of the row). It is no longer suggested at this store, for the whole family, until someone adds it again.
  • After adding, the keyboard closes. If the list is set to “Open item details”, the details of what you just added open (without the keyboard): set the quantity, note, priority or due date, then tap the floppy disk; the cross closes them without changing anything, and the row stays added.
  • Edit: tap the row. The item details open; the floppy disk saves.
  • In a task list, the details keep the name, the note and the priority, then three rows that sum up “Due date” (date, time, reminder), “Repeats” and “For”. Tap a row to open its page inside the details; the “Back to details” arrow, the phone's Back gesture or a tap outside the details take you back. Nothing is saved when you go back: once you are back on the details, the floppy disk saves everything; the cross discards everything. A task has no quantity.
  • Check: tap the box. A checked item is crossed out and moves to “In the cart” (“Done” in a task list), at the bottom of the list.
  • Delete: swipe the row to the left, then tap “Delete”. “Undo” brings the row back for a few seconds.
  • All at once, at the bottom of the list, after you confirm: “Put all back on the list” unchecks the items in the cart, “Clear the cart” deletes them for everyone. In a task list: “Reopen all” and “Remove done items”; in a wish list: “Reopen all” and “Remove received”.

5.2 Time and reminder

In a task list where the due date is turned on (8.3 Owner: editing the list), a task that has a date can also have a time: “Time (optional)”, in its details. “Clear” removes the time; clearing the date removes the time too.

  • On the row, the badge shows the date and the time: “Today · 6:30 PM”, “Tomorrow · 9:00 AM”.
  • A task with a time becomes “Overdue” as soon as the time has passed, without waiting for the next day. Without a time, nothing changes: it becomes overdue the next day.
  • On the same date, tasks with a time come before the others, in order of time (“By due date” sorting and the “Due” screen).
  • A repeating task keeps its time every time (5.3 Repeating tasks).
  • The public link shows the time, never the reminder.

Once the time is chosen, “Reminder” offers: “None” (to start with), “At the time”, “15 min before”, “30 min before”, “1 hour before”, “2 hours before” or “The day before (24 hours)”. Each task has its own reminder.

  • The reminder is a notification (6. Notifications): it shows up on the Notifications page, and on your devices where notifications are turned on. It goes to the person the task is assigned to (5.4 Assign a task); if it is assigned to nobody, to all the members of the list.
  • It is sent only once. If you change the date, the time or the reminder, it will be sent again at the new moment. A checked task sends nothing.
  • It follows your settings: “Due” notifications turned on, lists whose notifications you muted.
  • If the server was stopped at the planned moment, the reminder is still sent within the next half hour; after that, it is dropped.
  • If notifications are turned off on the server, the reminder only shows up on the Notifications page.

5.3 Repeating tasks

In a task list where the due date is turned on (8.3 Owner: editing the list), the details of a task offer “Repeats”: “Never”, “Every day”, “Every week”, “Every 2 weeks”, “Every month” or “Every year”.

  • Every week, or every 2 weeks: tap the days you want (Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun), at least one. For example, Monday and Thursday to take out the trash.
  • Every month: choose the day (the 5th of every month). The 31st falls on the last day of shorter months.
  • Or, with “A day of the week”: the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th or last Monday, Tuesday… of the month (“The 1st Monday of every month”, “The last Friday of every month”). To start with, Listo suggests the one of the due date.
  • Every year: the same day and the same month as the due date.
  • A sentence under the choice sums up the rule, for example: Every Monday and Thursday. If the task does not have a due date yet, Listo sets the first date by itself.
  • On the row, a badge shows the repeat (for example ↻ Mon, Thu).

When you check a repeating task, it does not go to “Done”: it comes right back, unchecked, at its next date. A message says “Next time:” and the date; “Undo” puts back the old date. A task done late comes back at the next upcoming date, never already overdue. It works the same from “Due” and offline.

“Skip this time”, in the details, moves on to the next date without checking the task (a week when there is nothing to do). The others see it as a change.

To stop: “Repeats”, “Never” (or clear the due date, or delete the task).

5.4 Assign a task

In a task list, the owner turns on “Assignment” in “Options for items” (8.3 Owner: editing the list). The details of a task then offer “For”: “Nobody”, “Me”, another person who can edit the list, or “Several people…”.

  • On the row, a badge says whose task it is: “Me” (in blue) or the first name.
  • “Sort list”, “By person”: the tasks of “Me” first, then those of each person, then those of “Nobody”.
  • “Due” offers “All” or “Mine” (5.11 Getting around: Home, Due).
  • A repeating task keeps the same person when it comes back (except with “Take turns…”).
  • If the person leaves the list, is removed from it or is switched to “Read only”, their tasks go back to “Nobody” (with several people or taking turns: they are removed from the group or from the rotation; if only one person is left, the task is theirs alone).
  • The public link never shows whose task it is.

“Several people…”: tap the first names (at least two). The task belongs to each of them: it appears in each person's “Mine”, counts in their 8 AM summary and sends them its reminders. The first one to check it completes it for everyone. On the row: “Me, Léa”, or “Léa +2”.

“Take turns…”, for a repeating task (5.3 Repeating tasks): tap the first names in turn order (1, 2, 3…). The task belongs to one person at a time (the badge shows their first name). When someone checks it, it comes back at its next date for the person who comes next in the order after the one who checked it: if Marc does Léa's turn, the next one is for Zoé. The message says “Next time: Mon, Nov 17 · Zoé”. “Skip this time” keeps the same person. If the task no longer repeats, it stays with this time's person.

When someone assigns you a task, or when it is your turn, the “Changes” notification tells you (‘It's your turn in “Home”’) (6. Notifications).

5.5 Type the quantity with the name

In a shopping list (only), write the quantity at the start of the name, or at the end with an x: Listo puts it in the “Quantity” field.

  • 6 eggs: eggs, quantity 6.
  • 2 lbs of apples, or 2 lbs apples: apples, quantity 2 lbs. Same with 500 g flour, 1.5 L milk, 3 packets of yeast or 4 boxes of rice.
  • eggs x6: eggs, quantity 6.
  • When in doubt, everything stays in the name: 7up, pie 6 slices, 2 in 1 shampoo. So that a number is never read, write it after the name (shampoo 2 in 1).
  • It also works with several items at once (6 eggs, 2 lbs apples; milk). If Listo got it wrong, fix it in the item details.
  • In a task list, the number is part of the name (3 calls to make): nothing is read; the quantity is set in the details. No quantity in a wish list.

5.6 Add several items at once

In a shopping or task list, separate the names with commas, semicolons or line breaks, then tap “+”: for example, milk, bread; 6 eggs adds three rows. You can also paste a list copied from your notes.

  • A comma between two digits does not split anything: 1,5 L of milk stays a single row.
  • 20 rows at most at a time. Beyond that, or if a name is too long, nothing is added and a message says so.
  • In a shopping list, Listo asks for the aisle of each new item, one after the other. If you close one of these windows, nothing is added and the text stays in the field.
  • With several rows, the list stays on screen, even when it is set to “Open item details”. This also works without a network.
  • In a wish list, the text is never split: one wish at a time.

5.7 Scan a barcode

In a shopping list, the barcode button next to the add field (“Scan a product”) opens the phone's camera or photo library. Take a picture of a product's barcode: Listo reads it on the phone (the photo is never sent).

  • An item on the list already has this code: Listo offers to check it.
  • An item on another of your lists has it: Listo reuses its name (“Known product”).
  • Otherwise, Listo looks up the product name in Open Food Facts; you can fix it, then tap “Add to list”.
  • This button only exists if scan mode is turned on on your server (setting LISTO_PRODUCT_LOOKUP, 3.3 Settings (variables)).

5.8 Add a photo

In an item's details, “Add a photo” takes a photo or picks one. It is made smaller on the phone before it is sent, and its hidden information (location, device) is removed by the server. The photo is optional; only the members of the list see it (and the visitors of a valid public link).

5.9 Groups, sorting and counter

  • Tap the header of an aisle (or of a store, or of a priority) to collapse or expand it. The chevron at the end of the row under the list title (“Collapse all”, then “Expand all”) acts on all the groups, and on subtasks (5.21 Subtasks).
  • “Lists” can be sorted by date or alphabetically (button at the top).
  • On the phone, under the “Lists” title, “All”, “Shopping”, “Tasks” or “Wishes” shows only the lists of that type. Your last choice is remembered on this phone. On a computer, the type is chosen in the menu on the left (5.20 On a computer).
  • In a shopping list, “Sort list” offers “By aisle” (“By store” for a “Multi” store) or “Alphabetical”: the items are then shown in one flat list, under “To buy”. Each item keeps its aisle.
  • In a task list, “Sort list” offers “Alphabetical”, “By priority”, “By due date” and “By person”, depending on the list's options.
  • The sorting you choose applies only to you, on this phone: it changes nothing for the others, and it is allowed with read-only access.
  • “X/Y” gives the number of items left out of the total, on each card and at the top of each group.
  • In an open list, under the title, a small bar shows the share already checked, followed by the same “X/Y” (on a computer, ‘5/6 left’). When everything is checked, the bar turns green and a check mark is added. Searching and collapsed groups do not change these numbers.

5.10 Search

  • Tap the round magnifying glass, at the bottom right: the field opens under the title, ready to type in, and does not move when the keyboard opens. In a list, it floats above the add bar, and hides while you type in it.
  • In a list, “Search this list” searches item names, not notes. The add bar hides during the search.
  • On “Lists”, “Search lists” searches the titles of the lists of the chosen type (“All” to see everything), including those shared with you.
  • On “Due”, “Search tasks” searches task names.
  • Capital letters and accents do not matter: ‘eclair’ finds ‘Éclair’. The text found is highlighted, and the number of results is shown.
  • Checked items that are found stay visible, crossed out, in “In the cart”. You can check, open or delete an item from the results.
  • The keyboard's ‘search’ key closes the keyboard and keeps the search; “Clear search” empties it. To close it: the cross in the field, another tap on the magnifying glass or the phone's Back button; if you tap elsewhere without having typed anything, it closes by itself. Switching lists or leaving the screen also empties it.
  • The search changes nothing in the list or for the others. It works offline, and with read-only access too.

5.11 Getting around: Home, Due

Listo opens on “Home” (unless you have turned it off, see below): ‘Hello’ and your first name, today's date, the status badge and the notification bell (with the number of unread ones: 6.1 The Notifications page), then tiles that show at a glance what is waiting. They work offline and update by themselves.

  • “Due” tile: “Overdue”, “Today” and “Later”, with the number of tasks, the same as on the “Due” screen (with its “All” or “Mine” choice). A row opens “Due” at that spot; with no task to do: “No due tasks”.
  • “Lists” tile: one row per type of list you have (“Shopping”, “Tasks”, “Wishes”), with the number of lists, and a blue dot if one of them has new updates. A row opens “Lists” on that type; with no list: “No lists” and “New list”.
  • “To give” tile, full width, only if you reserve a wish or chip in on a gift: the number of gifts still to buy (at the end of the title, which opens the “To give” page: 5.15 To give: your gifts) and the last three reserved and not yet bought (‘For Zoé · reserved’); a row opens the wish in its list. When everything is bought: “All bought”.
  • “Notes” tile: the number of your notes (at the end of the title), the last two changed, four on a computer (a row opens the note) and “New note”, which opens a new note (5.12 Notes); with no note: “No notes”.
  • “Account” tile: “My account”, “Notifications” (the Notifications page, like the bell) and “Settings”. For the Admin, when signing in with an identity provider is turned on, “Account requests” appears with their number when some are pending.
  • A tile's title opens the whole screen. An unknown address also leads to Home (Home turned off: to the first screen you chose); a link to a list always opens that list.

On the phone, the bar at the bottom leads to the main screens: “Home”, “Due”, “Lists”, “Notes” and “Settings” (four buttons, without “Home”, when the Home screen is turned off; six, with “Projects”, when you turned projects on: 7.1 Turning projects on); it sits just above the line at the bottom of an iPhone. It is not on Home, whose tiles already lead everywhere. When all of Home fits on the screen, it doesn't scroll (or bounce); otherwise, it scrolls as usual. The round magnifying glass, just above it on the right, opens the search (not on “Settings”). To create a list: “+”, at the top right of “Lists”.

  • “Due” gathers the tasks to do that have a due date, from all your task lists where the “Due date” option is turned on: “Overdue”, “Today”, “Tomorrow”, “This week”, then “Later”. Never wish lists or shopping lists. It can also show a project's dated cards, if you chose so for that project (7.7 A project's cards in “Due”).
  • Each row shows the task, its list, its priority, its date and its time. Tap the box to check it (if you can edit the list): it leaves the screen, and “Undo” brings it back; a repeating task stays, at its next date (5.3 Repeating tasks). Tap the row to open the task in its list.
  • If one of your lists has assignment turned on, “All” or “Mine” chooses what is shown; “Mine” keeps only the tasks assigned to you (5.4 Assign a task). The choice is remembered on the device.
  • “Due” shows two numbers: in red, at the top left of the icon, the overdue tasks; in green, at the top right, today's tasks. Each one only appears when there are some. Tasks assigned to someone else don't count. The cards of the projects shown in “Due” count the same way (yours and unassigned ones).
  • The screen works offline, like the lists. On a computer, “Due” is in the menu on the left, with the same numbers at the end of the row (red, then green).

Choosing your tiles, or turning Home off

Settings, “Home” (the first row of “General”) sets up the Home screen for your account: the choice applies to all your devices, and also works offline.

  • “Tiles shown”: one switch per tile, to hide or show it (“To give” only appears anyway when you have reserved a gift). The “Account” tile is always shown: there is no bottom bar on Home, and it is the tile that leads to Settings.
  • “Reorder”: drag a tile by its handle, or use the up and down arrows; “Done” closes this mode. Home follows this order; on the phone, a tile left alone on its row takes the full width.
  • “Show the Home screen” turned off: Listo opens on the “First screen” you chose (“Due”, “Lists”, “Notes”, or “Projects” when they are on; “Lists” at first), and the “Home” button leaves the bottom bar. The notification bell then moves to the top of “Due”, “Lists” and “Notes” (6.1 The Notifications page). On a computer, the “Home” entry of the menu on the left goes away, and the logo leads to the first screen you chose.

Choosing your features

Settings, “Features” (just below “Home”) chooses the features you use: notes (on at first) and projects (off at first: 7. Projects). A feature you turn off disappears from your screens, for you only; nothing is deleted. The choice applies to all your devices, and also works offline. Only the features the Admin allowed for your account are offered (4.3 Account roles: Admin, User, Guest).

  • Turn notes off: no more “Notes” button in the bottom bar (four buttons, or three if Home is also turned off), no more “Notes” tile on Home or in Settings, “Home”, no more “Notes” tab in the “Trash” in Settings; on a computer, no more “Notes” entry or “New note” in the menu on the left (the N key always opens “New list”). A link to a note leads to the first screen; if “Notes” was your first screen, Listo opens on “Lists”.
  • With notes turned off, you get nothing from notes: no new updates, no notifications (Notifications page and phone). Other people can still share a note with you; nothing changes for them.
  • Turning notes back on brings everything back: your notes, the ones shared with you, your pins and the notifications already received.

5.12 Notes

A note keeps free text: a code, a recipe, ideas. At first it is yours alone: nobody else sees it, not even the Admin; you can share it (5.13 Sharing a note). The text keeps its line breaks and can be formatted: headings, bold, lists, checkboxes, links (see “Formatting” below); web addresses in http or https become links. Don't use notes? Turn them off for yourself in Settings, “Features” (5.11 Getting around: Home, Due): they disappear from your screens, and nothing is deleted.

  • “Notes” screen (bottom bar, or the “Notes” tile on Home): “Pinned” notes first, then “Other notes”, most recently changed at the top. The sort button at the top (as in “Lists”) switches between most recent first and alphabetical order. The magnifying glass searches titles, texts and label names.
  • “+”, at the top right, opens a new note: the cursor goes straight into the text. On Home, “New note” does the same.
  • A note saves itself: one second after your last keystroke, when you leave the note and when Listo goes to the background. The line under the bar says “Saved”, “Saving…” or “Offline · saved on this phone”; offline, the note is sent when the network comes back.
  • To write in a note, tap its text: the cursor goes to that spot. A link opens in a new tab, and a checkbox gets ticked.
  • A note's ⋮ menu (on its card, or in the note): “Pin” or “Unpin”, “Share” (your notes: with people or by a link) or “People” (a note shared with you), “Labels” (if you can edit it), “Duplicate” (a copy of your own, not shared, whose title ends with ‘(copy)’), “Delete” (your notes) or “Leave”.

Formatting

A note's text can have headings, bold, lists or checkboxes. These are small marks written in the text (Markdown ones, if you know it): while you write, you see them as they are; as soon as you stop writing, the note is shown formatted, without the marks.

  • The formatting bar writes the marks for you. On the phone, it is at the bottom of the note, and just above the keyboard when it is open; on a computer, at the top of the text. It only appears if you can edit the note. Its ten buttons, in order: “Heading” (H1), “Subheading” (H2), “Bold” (B), “Italic” (I), “Underline” (U), “Highlight” (ab), “Bulleted list”, “Numbered list”, “Checkbox” and “Insert a link”.
  • A button applies to the chosen text, even without the keyboard: select words in the displayed note, then tap the button. Tapped a second time, it removes the mark; “Subheading” on a heading turns it into a subheading. Line buttons (headings, lists, checkboxes) apply to each chosen line. With nothing chosen, a button starts writing at the end of the note, already formatted.
  • The marks, to type yourself if you prefer: # Heading and ## Subheading at the start of a line; **bold**, *italic* (or _italic_), ++underline++ and ==highlight== around words; - (or * ) at the start of a line for a bullet, 1. for a numbered list (the numbers stay the ones you type); - [ ] for a checkbox, - [x] for a ticked checkbox. ++underline++ and ==highlight== aren't standard Markdown, but many note apps know them.
  • Checkboxes: in the displayed note, tap a checkbox to tick or untick it; the note saves as if you had typed. A ticked checkbox is also crossed out. With “Read only” and on the public page, checkboxes show but can't be ticked.
  • Links: “Insert a link” asks for the “Text to show” (already filled with the chosen text) and the “Address”, then “Insert” writes [text to show](https://…); the note only shows the text, which opens the address. Only http or https addresses are accepted: any other (javascript:, data:…) stays text, never a link. An address typed on its own (https://…) stays clickable.
  • A mark that isn't closed shows as it is (**bold stays **bold), and a line has only one start mark (heading, bullet, number or checkbox). Nothing else is interpreted, not even HTML: it stays text. A note's title is never formatted.
  • Elsewhere, the text reads without its marks: cards on the “Notes” screen, the Home tile, notifications, the title of a copy; search finds the text with or without its marks. A note's public page shows it formatted. An AI assistant reads and writes the text with its marks (9.6 Notes).

Labels

A label sorts a note: ‘Recipes’, ‘Holidays’, ‘Home’… Labels are shared by the whole household: everyone can create some and pick from other people's. A name exists only once, ignoring capitals and accents: ‘Home’, ‘home’ and ‘HOME’ are the same label.

  • Putting labels on a note: “Label” with its “+”, under the note's title (or the note's ⋮ menu, “Labels”). Tick the ones that fit, 10 at most per note; untick to remove. This also works offline.
  • Creating a label: type its name in the field of the same window. If that name already exists, Listo offers it instead of creating a second one; otherwise, choose one of eight colors, then “Create”. Creating needs a connection (30 characters at most).
  • A note's labels are the same for everyone who sees it. The owner and “Can edit” change them; “Read only” only sees them. Changing labels is quiet: nothing new and no notification for the others, and the note does not show ‘Edited by Léa’.
  • On the “Notes” screen, a note's labels are at the bottom of its card, to the left of the people's badges. Above the cards, “All” and the labels of your notes act as a filter: tap one to see only its notes (the choice is kept on this device, and combines with the search).
  • Only the Admin renames, recolors or deletes a label (Settings, “Administration”, “Labels” tab: 8.1 Admin). A note's public page never shows its labels.

Two people at the same time

If the note was saved elsewhere (by someone it is shared with, or on another of your devices) while you were writing, nothing is overwritten. A banner warns you (‘Léa just edited this note’); when it saves, Listo offers “Keep mine”, “Take theirs” (after confirmation) or “Keep both” (your text becomes a new note of your own). “Decide later” leaves the note “Needs checking”: your text stays on the device and the choice comes back when you reopen the note. If you are reading the note without writing, it updates before your eyes.

Trash

Your deleted notes go to the “Trash” in Settings, “Notes” tab (next to the “Lists” tab): they stay there for 30 days, then they are deleted for good (you never see other people's notes there). “Restore” puts it back in its place (pinned if it was); “Delete for good” and “Empty the trash” ask for confirmation. A shared note disappears for everyone; restored, it comes back for each of them, with their pin. Offline, all this waits for the network to come back.

On a computer

“Notes” is in the menu on the left, with the number of notes (and a dot when there's something new); on the notes screens, “New note” (N key) replaces “New list” at the bottom of the menu. A card opens its note in place of the cards, alone in the middle, like an open list: at the top, “Back to notes” (the arrow on the left), the “Saved” status, “Share” or “People”, the pin and the ⋮ menu; below, the title and the text, in lines short enough to read easily. It saves itself (offline: “Offline · saved on this device”). The arrow, Esc (when you're not typing) or the browser's back button take you back to the cards. On the “Notes” screen, the “/” key searches the notes.

5.13 Sharing a note

In one of your notes, “Share” (at the top of the note, or ⋮ menu) opens the same choice as for a list (8.4 Owner: sharing and choosing permissions). “With people on Listo”: check a person from the existing accounts, choose “Edit” or “View” at the end of their row, or uncheck them to remove them, then “OK”. “With a temporary link”: the public link (below). You need a network connection.

  • Owner: everything, including deleting the note, sharing it and managing its public link.
  • “Can edit”: change the title and the text. Never delete the note, share it or manage its public link.
  • “Read only”: read the note (links open), pin it for yourself. The card shows it with the “Read only” eye.
  • Everyone except the owner can leave the note: ⋮ menu, “Leave”. Someone who loses access also loses their pin. Each person's pin is never shown to the others.
  • The cards of a shared note show the people's initials; “People”, at the top of the note or in its card's ⋮ menu, lists them with their access. At the bottom of the note: ‘Edited by Léa · 5 min ago’.

What's new and notifications

When someone else changes a shared note, its card shows a dot and ‘Edited by Léa’ until you open it (it then counts as read on all your devices); the “Notes” tile on Home and, on a computer, the “Notes” entry in the menu on the left show the dot too. It's the same “What's new” setting as for lists (5.16 What's new); the note's ⋮ menu: “Hide what's new”.

Notifications (6.3 What you get, and when), on the Notifications page and on your devices: ‘Léa edited the note “Travel”’ (at most one per note every quarter of an hour) and ‘Léa shared the note “Travel” with you’. Without details, only the note's title is named, never its text; with details, the start of the text follows (6.4 Details in the notification). The Notifications page only names the title, never the text. The note's ⋮ menu: “Turn off notifications”.

A note's public link

“Share” (at the top of one of your notes, or ⋮ menu), then “With a temporary link”: the same sheet as for a list (8.6 Owner: the public link): duration, optional password shown only once, “Stop sharing”. The public page shows the title, the formatted text (clickable links, checkboxes that can't be ticked) and the date it was changed, read only; never the people or the pins. Moved to the trash, the note can no longer be seen through its link; restored, the link works again if it hasn't expired.

5.14 Reserve a wish, chip in

In a wish list, any member can reserve a wish, even with “Read only”, except the person the list is for. You need a network connection.

  • Tap the wish, then “Reserve”. The row then shows who reserved it.
  • When you reserve, turn on “Open to others” so that others can join in on a group gift; otherwise, nobody else can reserve it.
  • On an open wish, “Chip in” adds you. “Leave” removes you; when the last participant leaves, the wish is free again.
  • Each participant can open or close the reservation with “Open to others”.
  • Listo only says who is chipping in: no amount, no share, no payment.
  • Editing a reserved wish does not remove the reservation.

5.15 To give: your gifts

“To give” is an automatic list: all the wishes you reserve or chip in on, in all the wish lists you are a member of. It is yours alone, and works offline.

  • On “Lists”, its card (light blue, “Automatic”) comes first among the wish lists, under “All” and “Wishes”, only if you have at least one reservation. ‘3/5’: 3 gifts still to buy out of 5. It is not a list: nobody can add to it, rename it, share it or delete it, and it is not counted among your lists. On Home, the “To give” tile leads there too.
  • Gifts are grouped by person (‘For Zoé’), with their list, ‘Reserved’ or ‘You're chipping in (2 people)’, and their price.
  • Check “Bought” once you have bought the gift: it is crossed out and moves to the bottom of its group. Only you see this box: not the person the gift is for, not the others, not the notifications, not an AI assistant (9.5 Wish lists: no reservations).
  • Tap the row (not the box) to open the wish in its list: that is where you cancel a reservation (5.14 Reserve a wish, chip in).
  • A gift leaves the list by itself when the person has marked it “Received”, when your reservation or participation ends, or when the wish or its list is deleted.

5.16 What's new

  • In a shared list, Listo points out what the others added or changed since your last visit. Your own actions and deletions are never pointed out.
  • On “Lists”, a badge on the card's icon gives the number of new updates (“30+” after more than a month away).
  • In the list, each new update is highlighted, with who made it (“Added by Marie”). It stays highlighted during the visit, until you tap the row; when you leave the list, everything counts as seen, on all your devices.
  • List menu ⋮: “Hide what's new”, then “Show what's new” to bring it back. Each person chooses for themselves, read-only access included. This is separate from the list's notifications (6.5 Turning off notifications for a list).
  • Settings, “Notifications”, “In the app” part: “What's new” turns off all of it; “Include checked items” (shown only when “What's new” is on) adds what the others check or uncheck.
  • On some phones, the icon of the installed app also shows their total. To be told without opening Listo: 6. Notifications.

5.17 Offline and sync

Without a network, you can keep adding, editing and checking, and writing in your notes (5.12 Notes). The changes are sent when the network comes back.

The badge at the top shows the state: “Up to date”, “Syncing…” or “Offline”, with the number of pending changes. Tap it to sync.

Sharing (a list or a note), the public link, duplicating, leaving a list or a note, the “Lists” tab of the trash, reservations, creating a store or an aisle, and Administration need a connection.

5.18 Light or dark theme

Settings, “My account”, “Appearance” section: “Light”, “Dark” or “Auto” (like the phone). The choice applies to this device. On a computer, it is in the same place: Settings, “My account”, “Appearance”.

5.19 Language: French or English

The choice applies to your account, on all your devices; the app reloads in the chosen language. Your notifications also arrive in this language.

The names of lists, items, aisles and stores are not translated: they are your data. This help, too, is in French and in English: it follows the language you chose.

5.20 On a computer

In a window at least 1,024 pixels wide (a computer, a tablet turned sideways), Listo switches to a two-area layout. Below that, you get the phone layout; switching from one to the other keeps the list open and closes any open windows.

  • On the left, the menu: “Search lists”, then “Home”, “Due”, “Lists” (with the number of lists) and “Notes” (with the number of notes), and at the bottom “New list” (“New note” on the notes screens), your account and Settings. The logo takes you back to “Home” (Home turned off: to the first screen you chose). The Home tiles are shown in three columns there.
  • The menu follows the screen: on “Lists”, in a list and on “To give” (Wishes highlighted), it also shows “Types” (Shopping, Tasks, Wishes, with their number of lists and the new-updates dot). A type shows only its lists in the middle; “Lists” shows them all. An open list highlights its type. Elsewhere (Home, Due, Notes, Settings…), the menu only has its fixed entries. While you search in the menu, the lists found are shown below.
  • In the middle, every screen (Home, Due, Lists, a list, “To give”, Notes, Settings, Help…) has the same width. At the top, on the title line: the status pill, the screen's buttons and the search; the line below holds the rest (counter, “All” / “Mine”, sort).

When you hover over a row (or when it has the keyboard focus), a pencil and a trash can appear one above the other; on a touch screen, they stay visible.

5.21 Subtasks

In a task list, the owner turns on “Subtasks” in “Options for items” (8.3 Owner: editing the list). A main task can then group subtasks: the list stays short, and each project has its steps.

  • At the end of a main task's row, “1/3” says how many subtasks are done. Tap it: the subtasks appear right below, indented. Tap it again to collapse them. The chevron at the end of the row under the list title (“Expand all”, then “Collapse all”) acts on all the main tasks (and on the groups and the done items). This device remembers what is expanded.
  • To add a subtask: expand the main task and tap “Add a subtask”; or open a task's details and tap “Add a subtask”. Enter adds it and leaves the field ready for the next one; Enter on an empty field closes it.
  • To put an existing task under another one: its details, “Subtask of”, then the main task. “None” takes it out again.
  • One level only: a subtask has no subtasks. A task that has subtasks, or that repeats, cannot become a subtask.
  • A subtask has everything a task has (note, due date, time, reminder, priority, assignment), except repeating.
  • Checking the main task checks all its subtasks; the message offers “Undo”. It never gets checked by itself, even when the last subtask is done. Unchecking a subtask, or adding one, unchecks the main task.
  • A repeating main task (5.3 Repeating tasks) comes back at its next date with all its subtasks unchecked: handy for a checklist you need to go through again.
  • Subtasks keep the order chosen by hand, whatever the list's sorting: to start with, the order in which they were added (a new one goes at the end). To change it: expand the main task and tap the two arrows to the right of “Add a subtask” (“Reorder”). Drag a subtask by its handle (the three lines on the left), or tap “Move … up” and “Move … down”; “Done” closes the mode. Each move is saved right away, offline included; if two people move things at the same time, the last move wins.
  • Checked subtasks stay in their place, crossed out: you can see how far along you are. A checked main task goes to “Done” with its subtasks. “Remove done items” does not remove the checked subtasks of a main task that is still to do.
  • Deleting a main task also deletes its subtasks; “Undo” brings them all back.
  • The search also finds subtasks: they are shown under their main task. In “Due” (5.11 Getting around: Home, Due), a subtask shows the name of its main task. The public link shows subtasks under their main task, read only.
  • Turning off “Subtasks” erases nothing: the tasks are shown in one flat list, and are grouped again if the option comes back. A duplicated list keeps its subtasks.

6. Notifications

Listo tells you about what concerns you: a change in a shared list, a reservation, a share, every morning the tasks for the day, and a task's reminder. Everything shows up on the Notifications page (the bell on Home), even with no phone signed up. On top of that, Listo can let you know on your phone or computer, without opening it: it's optional, the feature is off by default on the server, and then everyone chooses, device by device.

The Settings screen, “Notifications”, has two parts: “In the app” (what's new, badges and glow: 5.16 What's new) and “On this device” (notifications, described here). The kinds (“Notifications you get”) apply both to the Notifications page and to your devices.

6.1 The Notifications page

The bell, at the top of Home (Home turned off: at the top of “Due”, “Lists” and “Notes”), opens the Notifications page; its badge gives the number of unread notifications (9+ above 9). The “Notifications” row of the “Account” tile leads there too. At the top right of the page, the settings button opens Settings, “Notifications”.

  • Notifications are grouped by day (“Today”, “Yesterday”, then the date), newest first. An unread notification is in bold, with a blue dot; “3 unread” says so at the top.
  • Tapping a notification marks it as read and opens what it's about: the list, the task, the note or the due tasks. If the list was deleted or is no longer shared with you: “This list is no longer available”.
  • Opening the page marks nothing as read: “Mark all as read” does. Opening a list, a note or the “Due” screen (from a phone notification, for example) marks the notifications about them as read. Read on one device means read on all of them.
  • Marking as read without opening: swipe an unread notification to the right (green “Read” area); or its ⋮ button, “Mark as read”.
  • Clearing: swipe a notification to the left, then “Clear”; or use its ⋮ button (keyboard, screen reader). “Clear all”, at the bottom, asks for confirmation.
  • Notifications are kept for 30 days, 300 at most (the oldest ones go).
  • Changes made close together in the same list or the same note fit on a single line, which updates (‘Léa added 4 items to “Shopping”’) and becomes unread again.

It fills up even without phone notifications (feature off on the server, device not signed up): nothing is sent anywhere but your server to fill it. Offline, it shows the last copy received; “Mark all as read” and “Clear” go out when the network is back.

Same settings as the phone: a kind turned off in “Notifications you get”, or a list or a note whose notifications you turned off, doesn't show up on the page either. The page always gives the details (name of the task, of the wish); “Show details in the notification” only concerns the phone's screen. For a note, it only names the title, never the text.

6.2 Turning them on for a device

Settings, “Notifications”, “On this device” part, then the “Get notifications on this device” switch. The browser then asks for your permission: accept. It only asks at that moment, never on its own. “Send a test notification” checks that everything works.

  • Android: in Chrome (or another browser), or from Listo installed on the Home Screen (4.5 Installing Listo on your phone).
  • Computer: Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. The notification appears in the corner of the screen or in the system's notification center.
  • iPhone and iPad: first add Listo to the Home Screen (4.5 Installing Listo on your phone), then open it from its icon. In a plain Safari tab, the screen explains this and the switch stays grayed out. You need iOS 16.4 or later.

Each device is turned on separately: a device where you sign out no longer gets anything. Tapping a notification opens Listo on the list in question.

6.3 What you get, and when

Four kinds of notifications, each turned on or off in Settings, “Notifications”, “Notifications you get” section. These settings apply to the Notifications page and to all your devices.

“Changes”

When: Someone else adds, edits or checks something in a list shared with you, or assigns you a task. Changes made within a minute are grouped into a single notification per list. A shared note changed by someone else: at most one notification per note every quarter of an hour. Projects: new cards, a card assigned to you, comments and changes of a followed card (7.6 Projects' new activity and notifications).

Example: ‘Léa edited “Shopping”’, ‘Léa assigned you a task in “Home”’, ‘Léa edited the note “Travel”’

“Wishes”

When: Someone reserves a wish or chips in for a gift, in a wish list where you can see the reservations.

Example: ‘Marc reserved a wish’

“Sharing”

When: You're added to a list, a note or a project, or your access changes.

Example: ‘Léa added you to “Home”’, ‘Léa shared the note “Travel” with you’

“Due”

When: Every morning around 8 AM, a single summary of the tasks due today and overdue, if there are any (with the cards of the projects shown in “Due”: 7.7 A project's cards in “Due”). And a task's reminder, at the time chosen in its details (5.2 Time and reminder).

Example: ‘3 tasks due today · 1 overdue’, ‘Reminder: a task today at 2:00 PM’

You're never notified about what you do yourself (or about what an AI assistant does for you: 9. AI assistants (MCP)). Unchecking and deleting are not notified. The due-date summary only counts task lists where due dates are turned on, and never tasks assigned to someone else. A task's reminder goes to the person it's assigned to, otherwise to all the members of the list.

6.4 Details in the notification

“Content” section, “Show details in the notification”: off at first. When it's off, the notification only says who changed which list, or how many tasks are waiting for you. When it's on, it also names the items (‘Added: Milk, Bread · Checked: Milk’, ‘Marc reserved “Bike”’, the names of the first tasks of the day with their time, ‘Reminder: Dentist’). Best avoided if other people can see your phone's lock screen.

6.5 Turning off notifications for a list

The list's ⋮ menu: “Turn off notifications”. It no longer sends you anything, neither on the Notifications page nor on your devices, whatever its kind (due-date summary included). “Turn notifications back on” brings them back. Everyone chooses for themselves; it's forgotten if you leave the list. A shared note has the same choice in its ⋮ menu.

This is separate from “Hide what's new”, in the same menu (badges and glow, 5.16 What's new): you can keep what's new for a list without getting its notifications, and the other way around.

6.6 Limits and wish lists

  • At most 30 phone notifications per person per hour (the due-date summary doesn't count); the Notifications page has no such cap.
  • If you already have the list open on screen when someone changes it, you're not notified: you've seen it.
  • Wish list: the person it's for never gets anything, neither on their devices nor on their Notifications page, about a reservation or a contribution, nor what others add to their list. The surprise is kept. A visitor using the public link, without an account, never gets notifications.

6.7 What Apple, Google, Mozilla and Microsoft see

A notification goes through your device's service: Apple (iPhone, iPad, Safari on Mac), Google (Chrome, Android, Edge on Android), Mozilla (Firefox) or Microsoft (Edge on Windows). The text is encrypted for your device only: these services can't read it. They see the time, the size of the message, and the device that receives it.

This is the third exception to the principle ‘no data sent anywhere but your server’, along with the version check and scan mode (1.2 What Listo doesn't do). It only exists if the person who runs the server turns the feature on.

6.8 Turning the feature on on the server

For the person who runs the server

Off by default. To turn it on, uncomment the - LISTO_PUSH=on line in docker-compose.yml, then:

docker compose up -d

The due-date summary goes out around 8 AM in the server's time zone: set TZ (for example TZ=Europe/Paris, already in the shipped file), otherwise it arrives at 8 AM universal time (3.3 Settings (variables)).

On first start, Listo creates its notification keys in secrets/vapid.json, next to the secret key. Keep a copy of it along with the key (3.7 Keep the secret key safe). If this file is lost, Listo creates a new one: each device that already gave permission subscribes again by itself the next time it opens Listo; otherwise, just turn the switch back on.

The server must be able to reach the notification services over HTTPS (fcm.googleapis.com, push.services.mozilla.com, push.apple.com, notify.windows.com). When the feature is off, no key is created and no request goes out to them; the Notifications page, the morning summary and reminders still work, in the app only (6.1 The Notifications page).

6.9 Nothing shows up

  • The screen says notifications were blocked: allow them in your browser or phone settings (notifications for this site, or for the Listo app), then come back to Settings, “Notifications”.
  • On iPhone: Listo must be installed on the Home Screen and opened from its icon, not from Safari.
  • The list was already open on screen: nothing is sent for what you've seen.
  • A change arrives about a minute later (grouping); the due-date summary, only around 8 AM.
  • Firefox after an update: quit it completely and open it again, then “Send a test notification”.
  • On a computer, the system must also allow the browser's notifications, in addition to Listo in the browser. On Mac: System Settings › Notifications › Firefox (or Chrome, Edge, Brave…), ‘Allow notifications’ turned on, style ‘Banners’ or ‘Alerts’; macOS keeps it off as long as you've never answered its request. On Windows: Settings › System › Notifications, notifications turned on and the browser allowed in the list. In Firefox, also check that ‘Pause notifications until Firefox restarts’ is not checked (Settings › Privacy & Security › Permissions › Notifications).
  • On a computer, the screen says the browser couldn't register with its notification service: the browser must be able to reach this service over the Internet (Google for Chrome and Brave, Microsoft for Edge, Mozilla for Firefox). A DNS filter (AdGuard, Pi-hole…), an ad blocker (uBlock Origin…), a proxy, a firewall or a VPN can prevent it, even if other sites that are already registered keep getting their notifications. To find out what to unblock: open the filter's query log, turn on notifications in Listo, and allow the addresses refused at that moment; they are the ones of the browser's service, for example …googleapis.com or …google.com for Chrome and Brave, …notify.windows.com or …wns.windows.com for Edge, …push.services.mozilla.com for Firefox. With a blocker in the browser, turn it off for a moment on Listo to try. In Brave, you also need to turn on ‘Use Google services for push messaging’ in its settings (Privacy and security), then restart it. The message gives the details provided by the browser.
  • Also check the phone's ‘Do Not Disturb’ or ‘Focus’ mode, the kind of notification in “Notifications you get”, and that the list's notifications aren't turned off. The screen says “Notifications on devices are turned off on this Listo server”: see 6.8 Turning the feature on on the server. In any case, the Notifications page keeps what you would have received (6.1 The Notifications page).

7. Projects

A project sorts cards into columns, like a board of ideas: ‘Ideas’, ‘Drafts’, ‘Review’, ‘Published’… Each project has a single board: its columns (a name and a color, in the order you choose), and in each column its cards, in the order you choose. For several boards, create several projects. A project is shared with Listo people, each with their role (7.9 Sharing a project, the four roles); it has no public link.

The feature is off at first, for each account: nothing changes until you turn it on.

7.1 Turning projects on

Settings, “Features”, “Projects”: turn the switch on. The choice is yours alone, on all your devices, and also works offline. Turned off again, projects disappear from your screens without deleting anything: your projects and the ones shared with you come back when you turn them on again.

  • On the phone, the bottom bar then has six buttons: “Home”, “Due”, “Lists”, “Notes”, “Projects” and “Settings” (five if Home or notes are turned off). With text enlarged by the phone, a label can be cut short (‘Du…’): the name read by the screen reader stays complete.
  • Home gets a “Projects” tile, at the bottom, across the full width: the number of your cards (assigned to you, not done), and the three most urgent (overdue first, then by due date); below, the project, the column and the due date (‘Overdue’ in red). A row opens the card; with no card for you, “No cards for you”. The title opens the “Projects” screen. Like the other tiles, it can be hidden and moved in Settings, “Home”; “Projects” can also become your first screen when Home is turned off.
  • The “Trash” in Settings gets a “Projects” tab (7.10 Project trash).

7.2 Create, duplicate, delete a project

  • “Projects” screen: one card per project, the most recently changed at the top, with its columns (colored dot, name, number of cards), the people's badges, the “Read only” eye or “Contributor” depending on your role, ‘2 cards for you · 1 overdue’ (your cards not done), and a dot when someone else did something there since your last visit. The magnifier searches for a project by its name.
  • “+”, at the top right (“New project” when you have none): give a name (80 characters at most), then “Create”. The project starts with three columns, “To do”, “In progress” and “Done”, which you can rename as you like; it opens right away.
  • A project's ⋮ menu: “Open”, “Share” (your projects) or “People”, “Project settings” (owner and “Can edit”, 7.8 The project's columns and labels), “Duplicate project”, “Deleted cards” (7.10 Project trash), “Turn off notifications” (7.6 Projects' new activity and notifications), “Hide new activity” (shared project), “Show in Due” (when the project's “Due dates” option is on, 7.7 A project's cards in “Due”), then “Delete” (your projects) or “Leave project”. In an open project, the ⋮ menu at the top is the same, without “Open”.
  • “Duplicate project” (any member, even “Read only”): a new project of yours, not shared, whose name ends with ‘(copy)’, with the same columns, the same labels and the same “Priority” and “Due dates” options, but without any cards. Handy to make a template. It also works offline.
  • “Delete” (owner): the project goes to the “Trash” in Settings for 30 days, with its cards; it also disappears for the people it is shared with. “Cancel”, in the message that follows, puts it back right away.

7.3 Cards

On the phone, a project shows one column at a time. At the top, the column tabs (colored dot, name, number of cards): the chosen column has its color as background, an outline and a bold name. Tap a tab, or swipe the screen sideways (starting from the middle, not the left edge) to go to the next column. Listo remembers, on this device, the last column opened in each project.

  • Add a card: type its title in the bar at the bottom (it names the column), then “+”; the card goes to the bottom of the column, and the field stays ready for the next one. No bar with “Read only”.
  • The magnifier searches the whole project (titles, descriptions, label names and link titles); while searching, the results are sorted by column.
  • “Filter” opens “Filter cards”: check people (“Me”, each member, or “Nobody” for unassigned cards) and labels, then the ‘Show 3 cards’ button; a card remains if it belongs to one of the checked people AND has one of the checked labels. “Clear” removes the filter. The filter is kept for this project, on this device; the number of filters is written on the button, and the tabs count the cards shown.
  • Move a card: a long press lifts it, then drag it to its new place in the column, or drop it on a tab: it goes to the top of that column. Without dragging, the open card has, in its ⋮ menu, “Move to…” (choose the column, then “At the top” or “At the bottom”, then “Move”), “Move up” and “Move down”.
  • Tapping a card opens it (7.4 An open card): its title (tap it to edit it, 200 characters at most), its column (the button in its color opens “Move to…”), its people, due date, labels, description and links. Its ⋮ menu: “Move to…”, “Move up”, “Move down”, “Duplicate” (a copy right below, yours), “Follow” or “Unfollow” (7.5 Comments, history, following a card) and “Delete”. Everything saves on its own, offline included.
  • A card your role doesn't let you edit says so (‘Read only’, or ‘You can read it, not edit it.’): it has no “+”, no typing, and no button that wouldn't work.

7.4 An open card

Each field of a card is always there, with its “+” or, when empty, a line to fill it (“Assign”, “Add a due date”…): there is only one place to fill it. Everything saves on its own, offline included. What your role doesn't allow has no button (7.9 Sharing a project, the four roles).

  • People: the badges and first names of the assigned people; “+” (or “Assign”) opens the choice, with “Assign to me” at the top. A card can be assigned to the owner, a “Can edit” or a “Contributor”, never to a person with “Read only” (10 at most). A person who leaves the project, is removed from it or becomes “Read only” is removed from their cards.
  • Due (when the project's “Due dates” option is on, which it is at first), on a single line: the “Done” box, then the date and time (tap them to change them). Without a due date: “Add a due date”. The time is optional; with a time, a reminder can be chosen (at the time, 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 2 h before, or the day before), written small below. “Remove due date” removes it, with the “Done” box.
  • The “Done” box is the only mark of a done card: the due date stays, in green, with a check mark. A done card is no longer overdue and has no reminder; it doesn't change column: put it away yourself (in ‘Published’, for example), or delete it. An overdue card has its date in red, with a triangle.
  • Labels: “Label” (or its “+”) adds or removes the project's labels (10 at most per card).
  • Priority (when the project's “Priority” option is on): “Low”, “Normal” or “High”. Turned off, the priority is kept without being shown.
  • Description: tap the text to write it (“Add a description” when it is empty); the same formatting as notes, with the formatting bar while typing (5.12 Notes). Checkboxes make a task list: ‘2/3’ above (boxes checked out of boxes), and a box is checked with one tap, without typing, even if someone checks another one at the same moment.
  • If someone changed the description while you were writing, nothing is overwritten: the card is “To check” and you choose “Keep mine”, “Take theirs”, “Keep both” (your text is added below theirs, after a ‘Version by Guillaume, Nov 25 9:04 PM’ line) or “Decide later”.
  • Links: a title (optional) and an address starting with http:// or https:// (20 links at most). Tap the title to open it in a new tab; ⋮: “Edit link”, “Copy address”, “Remove”. Listo never opens these addresses itself.
  • A Contributor on someone else's card assigned to them: they can edit everything on it and move it, and “Remove me” from the people; they can neither assign anyone nor delete it. Once removed, they can only read it.

On the card, in the column: its labels, then the due date (green and ‘Done’ when it is done, red with a triangle when overdue), the priority, an icon when it has a description, ‘2/3’ for its boxes, the number of links, the number of comments, the people's badges, and a blue dot when someone else changed it since your last visit. The screen reader reads all this with the title. Below the open card: the comments and the history (7.5 Comments, history, following a card).

7.5 Comments, history, following a card

Below an open card's links, two tabs: “Comments” (with their number) and “History”.

  • Write: “Write a comment…”, at the bottom of the screen on the phone (below the comments on a computer, Ctrl+Enter sends), then the arrow. The owner, “Can edit” and Contributors comment on every card; “Read only” reads the comments without being able to write any. A comment is plain text (4,000 characters at most); addresses starting with http:// or https:// become links. Offline included.
  • Your comments have “Edit” (‘edited’ is then written next to the date) and “Delete” (with confirmation). The project's owner can delete someone else's comment, never edit it.
  • A comment imported by an AI assistant (from another tool) is in a dashed bubble, with its original author's name and ‘imported by …’: it isn't linked to any Listo account (9.7 Projects).
  • History: what happened to the card, the most recent at the top, with who and when: created, moved (from one column to another), renamed, description changed, box checked, assigned, due date, “Done”, labels, links, comment added (without its text), deleted, restored; ‘(AI assistant)’ when the action comes from an assistant (9.7 Projects). It can only be read with the network; 300 lines at most per card.
  • Follow: you follow a card you created, that is assigned to you or that you commented on; its ⋮ menu (right column on a computer) has “Follow” for the others, and “Unfollow”, kept even if the card is assigned to you later. Following a card means getting its notifications (7.6 Projects' new activity and notifications).

7.6 Projects' new activity and notifications

  • New activity: when someone else created, changed, moved or commented on a card since your last visit, a blue dot says so on the project's card (“Projects” screen), on Home's “Projects” tile, in the menu on the left on a computer, and on each card concerned. Opening the project marks it as seen, on all your devices; the cards' dots stay during this visit. Project ⋮ menu: “Hide new activity”.
  • Notifications (Notifications page, and the phone when they are turned on there: 6. Notifications): a project's new cards (‘Léa added 3 cards to DomoPi Articles’, one line per project that updates), a card assigned to you, the comments and changes (moved, due date, “Done”, description) of a card you follow, grouped once per quarter of an hour, a card's reminder (with the “Due” notifications, to the assigned people, otherwise to everyone; never for a done card), and a project being shared.
  • Never what you do yourself. Without “Show details in the notification”, the phone doesn't name the card (‘a card’); a comment's text is never in a notification.
  • Project ⋮ menu: “Turn off notifications” (page and phone, reminders included), independent of “Hide new activity”.
  • Projects turned off (Settings, “Features”): no new activity or notifications from projects, except when a project is shared with you: the notification then invites you to turn projects on, and tapping it opens Settings, “Features”.

7.7 A project's cards in “Due”

A project's ⋮ menu: “Show in Due”, a switch, off at first (a check mark says when it is on); it is only there when the project's “Due dates” option is on (7.8 The project's columns and labels). It is your choice for this project only: Léa may want ‘Home repairs’ in her Due screen, and Guillaume not. Nobody else sees it; it also works offline, whatever your role.

  • When it is on, this project's dated cards that are not done appear on the “Due” screen (5.11 Getting around: Home, Due), mixed with the tasks, in the same groups (“Overdue”, “Today”, “Tomorrow”, “This week”, “Later”), with the projects icon and ‘DomoPi Articles · Review’ (the project and the column) instead of the list's name, then the priority (if the project's option is on), the date and time, and the people.
  • Tapping the row opens the card. The box checks “Done” if you can edit the card (7.9 Sharing a project, the four roles): the card leaves the screen, and “Undo” brings it back. Otherwise, the row has no box.
  • “Mine” keeps the cards assigned to you and the ones assigned to no one; “All”, all the dated cards of these projects.
  • The numbers on “Due” (bottom bar, menu on the left), Home's “Due” tile and the morning summary count them too (the summary then says ‘2 items due today · 1 overdue’ and opens “Due”). A project whose notifications you turned off stays on the screen, but is not in the summary.
  • A card's reminder doesn't depend on this choice (7.6 Projects' new activity and notifications). Projects turned off (Settings, “Features”): no cards in “Due”; the choice is kept for when you turn them back on.

7.8 The project's columns and labels

“Project settings” (⋮ menu of an open project; owner and “Can edit”):

  • Project name (the owner only).
  • Columns: one row per column, with its number of cards. Drag the handle on the left to change the order, or tap the pencil: name (40 characters at most, only once in the project), color among eight, position (before or after its neighbors) and “Delete column”. “Add a column” adds one at the end (20 at most).
  • Deleting an empty column: it goes right away. A column with cards: choose to move its cards to another column, or to delete them with it (they go to the project's “Deleted cards”). A deleted column can't be restored; a project always keeps at least one column.
  • Project labels: specific to this project (the notes' labels don't mix in), a given label existing only once (upper case and accents count the same). “New label”; the pencil renames, changes the color or deletes (the label is then removed from its cards). 50 labels at most per project.
  • “Priority” (the owner): a project option, off at first. Turned on, each card has a priority, “Low”, “Normal” or “High” (7.4 An open card); turned off again, priorities are kept without being shown.
  • “Due dates” (the owner and “Can edit”): a project option, on at first: a date, a time and a reminder on cards, and the “Done” box (7.4 An open card). Turned off, nothing is erased, but cards no longer have a due date, a reminder or a “Done” box: they are never overdue, no longer go to “Due” or the morning summary, and “Show in Due” leaves the ⋮ menu. Turned back on, everything comes back as it was.
  • “Delete project” (the owner).

7.9 Sharing a project, the four roles

In one of your projects, “Share” (the people button at the top, or the ⋮ menu) opens the list of Listo accounts directly: a project has no public link. Check a person, then choose their access under their name, “Edit”, “Contribute” or “View”; uncheck them to remove them; then “OK”. A person you add starts with “View”. It needs the network.

“Owner”

What it allows: everything: rename, share, delete the project, the “Priority” option, erase deleted cards for good

“Can edit” (“Edit” access)

What it allows: the columns, the project's labels, the “Due dates” option and all the cards; never rename, share or delete the project

“Contributor” (“Contribute” access)

What it allows: create cards; edit and move the ones they created or that are assigned to them; assign people on their own cards, and only remove themselves from someone else's card; delete and restore only the ones they created; never the columns or the project's labels

“Read only” (“View” access)

What it allows: see the project, its columns and its cards, without changing anything

  • Any member can duplicate the project, see “People” (who, and with which role) and leave the project (⋮ menu, “Leave project”; except the owner, who deletes it). What belongs to each person (hidden new activity, open column, filters) is never shown to the others.
  • Nobody else, not even the Admin, sees a project that isn't shared with them.
  • A changed role takes effect right away; an action made offline then refused by the server (the role changed in the meantime) is reported, and the screen goes back to the server's state.

7.10 Project trash

  • Deleted projects: “Trash” in Settings, “Projects” tab (the owner only). Each row says when the project was deleted, how many days it has left, its number of cards and how many people it was shared with. “Restore” brings it back for everyone, with its columns and cards; “Delete for good” and “Empty the trash” ask for confirmation and need the network. After 30 days, it is erased for good.
  • Deleted cards: in each project (⋮ menu, “Deleted cards”), because anyone can delete cards. Each row: title, original column, who deleted it and when. “Restore”: the owner and “Can edit” for all of them, a Contributor for the ones they created; the card comes back at the bottom of its column (or of the first one, if its own no longer exists). “Delete for good” and “Empty the trash”: the owner, with the network. With “Read only”, the list can be seen, without buttons.

7.11 On a computer

“Projects” is in the menu on the left (after “Notes”), with the number of projects; on the projects' screens, your projects are listed below it (number of cards, new-activity dot), and the bottom button becomes “New project”, or “New card” in a project (N key).

  • An open project takes the full width: at the top, its name, ‘Project · 5 columns · 55 cards’, “Share” (P key) and the search (/ key); below, the filters by person (“All”, “Me”, each member, “Nobody”) and by label (several choices; ‘+ 2’ shows the others; “Clear filters”); then the columns side by side, which scroll sideways.
  • Each column has its ⋮ menu (owner and “Can edit”): rename and change the color, move left or right, “Delete column”. “Add a card”, at the bottom of a column, opens a field (Enter adds, Escape closes); “Add a column” is at the end.
  • Drag and drop: hold a card with the mouse and drop it in the same column or another one; a dashed place shows where it will land. With the keyboard: the arrows go from card to card (up and down in a column, left and right from one column to the next), Enter opens the card, M opens “Move to…”. Right-click on a card: its menu.
  • A card opens in a window in the middle, over the project: on the left the title and ‘in Review · DomoPi Articles · created by Guillaume on October 2’, its description, its links, then the comments and the history; on the right, in this order, the column, the people, the labels, the due date (“Done” box and date on one line, reminder below), the priority (option on) and the actions (“Move to…”, “Duplicate”, “Follow” or “Unfollow”, “Delete”). The address is the card's: you can send it or come back to it. Escape or the cross close it.

8.2 Owner: creating a list

On “Lists”, tap “+” at the top right (on a computer: “New list”). Give it a title, choose the type of list (“Shopping”, “Tasks” or “Wishes”) and an icon.

  • Shopping list: also choose the store. Items are sorted by aisle, or by store for a “Multi” store.
  • Task list: “Options for items” turns on priority, due date and assignment.
  • “After adding” (all lists): “Stay on the list” to keep adding, or “Open item details” to fill in what you just added right away. To start with: “Stay on the list” for a shopping list, “Open item details” for the others.
  • If the store or aisle is missing, “New store…” or “New aisle…” creates it (network connection needed). An aisle created this way belongs to the list's store (to “All stores” for a list without a store).

8.3 Owner: editing the list

The list's ⋮ menu, “Edit”: the title, the type, the store, the icon. For a task list, “Options for items” turns priority, due date, assignment and subtasks on or off (5.21 Subtasks); for a wish list, “Priority”. “After adding” chooses between “Stay on the list” and “Open item details”. Only the owner edits the list.

  • Changing the type doesn't erase anything. A shopping list that becomes a task list hides its aisles, its store and its barcodes; a task list that becomes a shopping list hides priority and due date. Everything comes back if the list goes back to its original type. The form reminds you of this before saving.
  • Changing the store: each item keeps its aisle if it's also used in the new store, otherwise it goes to the aisle with the same name; failing that, it goes to “No aisle”. The “Put items in aisles” window then opens so you can choose an aisle right away (or later, from the item's details).
  • Priority (low, normal, high) is off to start with, for a task list as well as for a wish list. Everyone sees it. Due dates exist only for task lists.
  • Becoming a wish list, on the other hand, is permanent (8.5 Owner: wish list).

8.4 Owner: sharing and choosing permissions

The list's ⋮ menu (or its card's ⋯ menu), “Share”: a small choice opens, with two rows that tell you where things stand. “With people on Listo” shows who the list is shared with (‘Léa and Zoé’, or “Only you”); “With a temporary link”, the state of the public link (“No link”, ‘Active link · expires in 6 days’…, 8.6 Owner: the public link).

“With people on Listo” opens “Share with people”: one row per person. Check a person, then choose their permission at the end of their row; uncheck them to remove them. “OK” saves everything at once and takes you back to the choice; the cross closes without changing anything. You need a network connection.

  • “Edit” (“Can edit” permission): the person is a contributor (8.8 Contributor (“Can edit”)).
  • “View” (“Read only” permission): the person can only read (8.9 Read only). This is the starting choice.
  • You can change the permission or remove a person at any time.
  • The other members see “People” instead of “Share”: who the list is shared with, with which permission, without being able to change anything.

8.5 Owner: wish list

A wish list gathers gift ideas for one person, the recipient. The people close to them can reserve a wish (5.14 Reserve a wish, chip in). The surprise is kept: the recipient never sees that a wish is reserved, nor by whom, nor how many people are chipping in, even if they created the list themselves (8.11 Recipient of a wish list).

  • “Who is it for?” is chosen when the list is created: “Me”, another account, or “Someone without an account” (a child, for example). It can't be changed afterwards, and the list can't change type anymore.
  • A shopping or task list can become a wish list: in “Edit list”, choose “Wishes”, then “Who is it for?”. This is permanent, it needs a network connection, and nothing is erased: quantities and due dates are kept without being shown, checked items become “Received”, and an active public link is stopped.
  • If the list is for another account, that person is automatically part of it, with “Can edit”: they add their wishes themselves. In “Share with people”, their row is checked, “Recipient · Can edit”, and can't be unchecked.
  • A wish has a name and, if you want, a link (“Open link”), an “Approximate price” (in the currency set on the server, euros by default), a note and a photo.
  • Checking a wish moves it to “Received”: only the recipient and the owner can do this; reservations are no longer shown there.
  • When the list is for someone else, its owner sees who's chipping in and can “Remove” a participant or “Release” the wish (for example if someone has left the list). On a list for yourself (“Me”), nobody can remove a participant who has left (10.11 Wish list: a participant has left).
  • A wish list can't be copied (“Duplicate” isn't offered in its menu).

8.6 Owner: the public link

“Share” (⋮ menu), then “With a temporary link”, opens “Share with a temporary link”: “Create link” creates an address that you send to a person without an account. They see the list as read only (8.10 Public link visitor). A note has its own public link too, with the same rules: “Share” in its ⋮ menu (5.13 Sharing a note).

  • Duration: 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, “Custom” or “Unlimited”. “Change duration” changes it.
  • “Protect with a password”: the password is shown only once. Write it down.
  • “Stop sharing” makes the link unusable right away.
  • Wish list: the visitor can also reserve a wish or chip in on a gift. A reservation made through the link shows with the first name given, followed by ‘(public link)’; the owner can remove it, just like a reservation by someone close.

Don't give a wish list's link to its recipient: if they open it without being signed in to Listo, they'll see which wishes are reserved (never by whom). Signed in, they see nothing about reservations.

8.7 Owner: duplicating, deleting, restoring

  • “Duplicate” (the list's ⋮ menu) creates a copy in your name, with or without its items (all unchecked). The copy is private: the people the list is shared with, the public link and the photos are not copied.
  • The list's ⋮ menu, “Delete”: a confirmation first reminds you what the list contains and who it's shared with. The list then disappears for everyone, and its public link stops.
  • A deleted list stays in your “Trash” (Settings, “Lists” tab) for 30 days, then it's erased for good. Only its owner sees it there; each row shows its type, its number of items, the day it was deleted and the number of days left.
  • The “Trash” has two tabs, “Lists” and “Notes” (your deleted notes: 5.12 Notes), and a third one, “Projects”, when you use projects (7.10 Project trash); each with its number when there are any. It opens on “Lists”, unless only notes (or projects) are waiting there; the chosen tab stays when you go back. If you turned notes off (Settings, “Features”) and projects are off too, it has no tabs: only your lists.
  • “Restore” puts it back the way it was, for everyone it was shared with: items, each person's permission, settings, photos, reservations. It needs a network connection. Its public link doesn't come back: create it again if needed.
  • “Delete for good” (a row's red trash can) erases the list right away, with its items and photos; “Empty the trash”, at the top, erases all the lists shown. Both ask for confirmation and need a network connection, and nothing comes back afterwards: the list can no longer be restored.
  • The owner doesn't leave their list: they delete it, or keep it.

8.8 Contributor (“Can edit”)

  • Can: check, add, edit and delete items and their photo (5. Everyday use); create a missing store or aisle; “Duplicate” the list (the copy belongs to them); reserve on a wish list (unless they're its recipient).
  • Can't: change the title, the type or the options, share, create a public link, delete the list.

Leaving: the list's ⋮ menu, “Leave”. The list disappears from your lists; your items stay in it. The owner can share it with you again. It needs a network connection.

8.9 Read only

The list's card and the top of the list show “Read only”.

  • Can: read the list, its items and their photos (tapping a row opens its details); sort and search; reserve a wish on a wish list; leave the list (⋮ menu, “Leave”).
  • Can't: add, check, edit or delete anything.

8.10 Public link visitor

A person without an account opens the link they were sent, without installing anything.

  • Can: read the list; tap an item to open its details (quantity, full note, aisle or store, checked or not, barcode, photo to enlarge, priority and due date if they're turned on, and a task's repeat).
  • Wish list: see the wishes (name, note, link, price, priority, photo) and those already received; “Reserve” a free wish, “Chip in” on a gift open to others, cancel their participation. They're asked for their first name once. They only see whether a wish is reserved, never by whom or how many.
  • Can't: edit the list, see who else has reserved.
  • The link may ask for a password, and it stops working when it expires or when the owner stops sharing (10.3 A public link doesn't work anymore).

8.11 Recipient of a wish list

The person the wish list is for adds their wishes (if they have an account) and marks them “Received”.

  • Never sees: that a wish is reserved, by whom, how many people are chipping in, or “Open to others”. Not in the list, not in What's new, not through an AI assistant (9.5 Wish lists: no reservations), and not on the public link page when signed in.
  • Can't reserve their own wishes.
  • Only someone with access to the server (database files, backups) could read a reservation: don't give this access to the recipient if the surprise matters.

9. AI assistants (MCP)

9.1 What it's for

An AI assistant is a program you chat with, for example Claude (Anthropic's assistant: claude.ai, the mobile app, Claude Desktop, Claude Code). Listo follows the MCP standard: any AI assistant that accepts a custom connector (or MCP server) can plug into it, such as Claude, ChatGPT or Mistral's Le Chat, depending on their versions and plans. Once it's connected, it can read your lists and, if you allow it, add items to them, check them and edit them, just by asking. It can also read your notes and, if you allow it, create notes, add to them or sort them with labels (9.6 Notes); and work in your projects: read their cards and, if you allow it, create cards, move them, or bring over a board from another tool (9.7 Projects).

The assistant acts with your account and your rights, never more: what it adds shows up under your name, as if you had done it yourself, and it only sees the lists you allowed it to. Nothing happens without your approval, given on Listo's “Authorize an AI assistant” screen.

This access only exists if the person who runs the server has turned it on (9.14 Access turned off at first). Otherwise, the “AI assistants” section doesn't appear in “My account”.

9.2 Example sentences

  • Read: ‘What are my Listo lists?’, ‘What's on the Groceries list?’.
  • Add, with an aisle: ‘Add tomatoes to the produce aisle in Groceries.’
  • Add, with a store (“Multi” list): ‘Add milk at Costco to the Saturday list.’
  • Check: ‘Check off the bread and the butter.’ Uncheck: ‘Uncheck the milk.’
  • Priority and due date (task list): ‘Make the Light bulb task high priority, due Friday.’
  • Time and reminder (5.2 Time and reminder): ‘Dentist Thursday at 2 pm, remind me an hour before.’ ‘No more reminder for the dentist.’
  • Assign (5.4 Assign a task): ‘Give the light bulb task to Léa.’ ‘What do I have to do this week?’ ‘The garage, for Léa and Marc.’ ‘Trash every Monday, taking turns: Léa, Marc, Zoé.’
  • Task that repeats (5.3 Repeating tasks): ‘Trash, every Monday and Thursday.’ ‘Rent, on the 5th of every month.’ ‘The meeting, on the first Monday of the month.’ ‘Stop repeating the watering.’ The assistant tells you the next date. If it checks a task that repeats, the task comes back on its next date, and the assistant tells you that date.
  • Rename or add details: ‘Rename milk to 2% milk’, ‘Add the note organic to the apples.’
  • Move to an aisle: ‘Put the milk in the dairy aisle.’
  • Subtasks (5.21 Subtasks): ‘Add the steps Batch 1, Batch 2 and Batch 3 under the Authelia task.’ ‘Put Bitwarden under the Accounts task.’ ‘Take Mac out of its main task.’ ‘Put Check backups first under Authelia.’
  • Notes (9.6 Notes): ‘What's in my Codes note?’ ‘Add some AA batteries to my To buy soon note.’ ‘Create a Gift ideas note with the Family label.’ ‘Put the Recipes label on the Crumble note.’ ‘Move the Draft note to the trash.’
  • Projects (9.7 Projects): ‘Which cards are assigned to me in DomoPi Articles?’ ‘Add a Proxmox 9 card in the Ideas column, for Léa, with the Proxmox label.’ ‘Move the 2FAuth card to Review.’ ‘Recreate my Planka Blog board in Listo.’

The assistant first reads the list to learn its aisles or stores, and it must never make any up. If an item was left without an aisle or a store, it must tell you. Exactly how it behaves is up to the assistant itself: check in Listo.

9.3 Two levels of access

When you connect an assistant, Listo's “Authorize an AI assistant” screen lets you choose:

  • “Read only” (selected at first): the assistant can read your lists, your notes and your projects, without changing anything.
  • “Read and write”: on top of that, the assistant can add items, check or uncheck them, and change their name, quantity, note, priority, due date (date, time, reminder), repeat, the person they're assigned to, their aisle or their store; and, for notes, create notes, add to them, replace their text, set their labels, create a label or move a note to the trash (9.6 Notes); and, in your projects, depending on your role, create and edit projects, columns, labels, cards and comments (9.7 Projects).

You also choose “All my lists, notes and projects” or “Some lists”. With “Some lists”, the assistant sees no notes and no projects at all. Even with “Read and write”, the assistant can only write where you yourself can write: if you have the “Read only” right on a list, a note or a project, it can only read it.

9.4 What an assistant can never do

Whatever the level of access, an AI assistant can never:

  • delete an item, or change its link or its price;
  • create, rename, delete or share a list;
  • manage people, the public link or the trash;
  • delete a note for good, restore it, share it or manage its public link;
  • rename, recolor or delete a notes label;
  • share a project, change a role, leave a project, restore or delete for good a project or a card, empty a trash;
  • see photos, or who added what.

9.5 Wish lists: no reservations

An AI assistant never receives any reservation data, for anyone: not who reserved, not even whether a wish is reserved. So the surprise is kept, even if the recipient asks their assistant. In a wish list, checking means ‘received’: only the recipient and the list's owner can do it, with or without an assistant.

9.6 Notes

An AI assistant sees your notes as you do (5.12 Notes): yours and those shared with you, with your right on each one, never other people's notes or your trash. It acts on your behalf: a note it adds to shows “Edited by” you, and the people of the note are told just as if you had done it yourself (5.13 Sharing a note).

  • Read: it sees the list of your notes (title, start of the text, labels, pin), then the text of a note.
  • Add to a note: it adds text at the end of a note, on a new line, without touching the rest. It's the safest way: nothing is lost, even if someone is writing in the note at the same time.
  • Formatting: it reads the text with its marks and can use them when writing (for example a checkbox, - [ ] AA batteries) (5.12 Notes).
  • Replace the whole text: only if the note hasn't changed since it read it; otherwise nothing is written and it has to read it again. It must ask you to confirm first.
  • Create a note: it's yours, shared with no one.
  • Labels: it only sets labels that already exist (capitals and accents ignored); with an unknown name, nothing is done. It can create a label if you ask it to.
  • Delete: only your notes, and only to the trash, where you can restore it for 30 days (5.12 Notes). It must ask you to confirm first; it can never delete it for good.
  • With the “Read only” right on a note, it can only read it, even with “Read and write”.

Notes turned off for you in Settings, “Features” (5.11 Getting around: Home, Due), or not allowed for your account by the Admin: the assistant can't reach them at all, and it tells you so. A connection limited to “Some lists” sees no notes: reconnect the assistant with “All my lists, notes and projects” (9.9 Connect an AI assistant).

9.7 Projects

An AI assistant sees your projects as you do (7. Projects): yours and those shared with you, with your role in each one (7.9 Sharing a project, the four roles), never other people's projects or your trash. It acts on your behalf: a card it creates is yours, the card's history adds ‘(AI assistant)’, and the people of the project are told just as if you had done it yourself (a single notification per project and per quarter of an hour, even for 50 cards).

  • Read: the list of your projects, then a project (people, columns, labels and cards), then a whole card (description, links, comments, and its history if you ask for it).
  • Projects: create one (it's yours, shared with no one), rename it or move it to the trash (owner only; you can restore it for 30 days, Settings, “Trash”, “Projects” tab), turn its options on or off: “Priority” (owner) and “Due dates” (owner and “Can edit”, 7.8 The project's columns and labels). It must ask you to confirm before moving it to the trash. On a project whose “Due dates” option is off, it sets no due date and no “Done”: it first offers to turn the option on.
  • The project's columns and labels: add them, rename them, change their color or their order, delete them (owner and “Can edit”). A column that has cards can only be deleted by saying where its cards go: the assistant never deletes a card that way.
  • Cards: create them (up to 50 at once, with their column, labels, people, due date and “Done”, description and links), edit them, move them, or put them in the project's “Deleted cards” (can be restored for 30 days). It must ask you to confirm before creating more than 20 cards, and before deleting any.
  • Comments: it writes them on any card, unless you have the “Read only” role.
  • It has exactly your rights: as a “Contributor”, it only edits and moves the cards you created or that are assigned to you, and only deletes the ones you created; with “Read only”, it can only read.
  • Columns, labels and people: it names them (capitals and accents ignored). With an unknown name, nothing is done: it lists the possible names for you. A person with “Read only” is never given a card.

Example: bringing over a Planka board. If your assistant can read your Planka board, ask it: ‘Recreate my Blog board in Listo, with its columns, labels, cards and their comments.’ It creates the project and its columns (with the closest colors), the labels, then the cards in batches, with people recognized by their first name, and finally the comments. A comment brought over keeps its author's name and its original date, and shows ‘imported by’ you: it isn't linked to any Listo account. The assistant never shares the project: you share it yourself afterwards, choosing each person's role (7.9 Sharing a project, the four roles).

Never: share a project, change a role, leave a project, restore, delete a project or a card for good, empty a trash, or touch your own settings (what's new, notifications, Due dates).

Projects turned off for you in Settings, “Features” (7.1 Turning projects on), or not allowed for your account by the Admin: the assistant can't reach them at all, and it tells you so; without “Can create their own projects”, it creates no project. A connection limited to “Some lists” sees no projects: reconnect the assistant with “All my lists, notes and projects” (9.9 Connect an AI assistant).

9.8 Limits

  • 20 items at most per request.
  • Per connection and per hour: 100 additions, 300 items checked or unchecked, 300 items edited, 100 note writes. Beyond that, the assistant gets a refusal and nothing is written.
  • Notes: 100 at most in one answer (the assistant can search or filter by label); a note holds 20,000 characters at most.
  • Projects: 50 cards, moves or comments at most per request; per connection and per hour, 1,000 cards created, edited, moved or deleted, 300 comments and 5 projects moved to the trash; 200 cards at most in one answer (the assistant reads the rest page by page).
  • 120 calls per minute per connection.
  • 5 active AI assistant connections at most per account; a connection unused for 30 days frees itself up.
  • The assistant doesn't create an item with the same name as one that's already there and not checked: it tells you instead.

9.9 Connect an AI assistant

The steps are the same for every assistant:

  1. In Listo: Settings, “My account”, “AI assistants” section, “AI assistants”. Under “Address to enter in your AI assistant”, tap “Copy address”. It looks like https://listo.exemple.fr/mcp.
  2. In your AI assistant: in its settings, add a custom connector (or MCP server) with this address. Menu names change from one assistant to another; see its help.
  3. The assistant opens Listo's “Authorize an AI assistant” screen. Check the website shown under “The response will be sent here” and that you're the one who just started the connection (9.11 Be careful: check the website), choose the level and the lists, enter your password (and your two-factor authentication code), then “Authorize”.

Example with Claude (claude.ai, the mobile app or Claude Desktop): in its settings, add a custom connector with Listo's address, then connect it; on the “Authorize an AI assistant” screen, the website shown is claude.ai.

With Claude Code, on a computer: claude mcp add --transport http listo https://listo.exemple.fr/mcp, then /mcp to authorize. The response then goes to “an app on this computer (localhost)”.

Signed in with an identity provider (4.10 Signing in with an identity provider): no password at this step. The screen first asks “Confirm with …”: the provider asks you to sign in again, then you come back to choose the level and the lists, and “Authorize”.

If you didn't start the connection yourself, tap “Deny”.

No “AI assistants” section in “My account”? The Admin hasn't allowed it for your account (4.3 Account roles: Admin, User, Guest): the “Authorize an AI assistant” screen says so too.

9.10 See and disconnect an AI assistant

Settings, “My account”, “AI assistants”: every authorized connection appears there, with the app, its right (“Read only” or “Read and write”), its lists, its date and when it was last used.

  • “Disconnect” cuts a connection right away; “Disconnect all” cuts them all. No password is asked.
  • Changing your password also disconnects all your AI assistant connections.
  • To reconnect an assistant, you have to authorize it again.
  • The Admin doesn't see other accounts' AI assistant connections; but disabling an account or resetting its password cuts them all.

9.11 Be careful: check the website

Any AI assistant that follows the standard can ask for access to Listo: you are the one who decides, on the “Authorize an AI assistant” screen. It shows in large type the website that will receive the response (for example claude.ai for Claude). Check that it really is your assistant's. If you didn't start the connection yourself, deny: someone may be trying to get into your lists.

When the app chooses its own name, it is followed by “name given by the app, not verified”: trust the website, not the name. The person who runs the server can also accept only some websites (9.15 Turn on access on the server).

9.12 Be careful: booby-trapped texts

A shared list, note or project can contain a booby-trapped text, written by someone else to trick the AI assistant (for example a note asking it to check everything). Listo always presents the content of lists, notes and projects to the assistant as data, never as instructions, but only choose “Read and write” if you need it.

Only ask the assistant to change an item, a note or a card yourself; check in Listo what it did.

9.13 Troubleshooting

  • After a Listo update, the assistant may keep its old list of tools: start a new conversation, or disconnect and reconnect the connector in the assistant.
  • A list is missing: the connection may only allow some lists, or you're no longer a member. Reconnect the assistant with “All my lists, notes and projects” (9.9 Connect an AI assistant).
  • The assistant doesn't see your notes: the connection is limited to “Some lists”, or notes are turned off for you in Settings, “Features” (9.6 Notes). After the update that added notes, start a new conversation so it knows its new tools.
  • The assistant doesn't see your projects: the connection is limited to “Some lists”, or projects are turned off for you in Settings, “Features” (9.7 Projects). After the update that added projects, start a new conversation so it knows its new tools.
  • The assistant says a column, a project label or a person is unknown: nothing was done; it should list the valid names for you. A person with “Read only” in the project can't be given a card.
  • The assistant says it can't edit a card: as a “Contributor”, you (and it) only edit the cards you created or that are assigned to you (7.9 Sharing a project, the four roles).
  • The assistant says a label is unknown: it doesn't exist yet. Nothing was done; ask it to create the label, or choose one of those it lists for you.
  • The assistant says the note has changed: someone edited it since it read it. Nothing was written; it has to read it again before trying again.
  • The assistant refuses to write: the connection is “Read only”, or you only have the “Read only” right on this list.
  • Nothing works anymore: the connection may have been cut (password changed, “Disconnect”). See “AI assistants” (9.10 See and disconnect an AI assistant).
  • Listo shows a page saying the authorization request is invalid: the assistant's request couldn't be verified (return address refused, incomplete request), or its website isn't accepted by the server (9.15 Turn on access on the server). Start the connection again from the assistant; if it happens again, tell the person who runs the server.
  • The assistant says an aisle or a store is unknown: it doesn't exist, it's disabled, or this list doesn't offer it (an aisle from another store, or a store in a “Multi” list). Nothing was added; the assistant should list the valid aisles or stores for you. Choose one, or ask the Admin to create it (Settings, “Administration”, “Shopping” tab).
  • The assistant says a store can't be chosen in this list: in a list with a “Single” store, the store is the list's own; you choose the aisle. The aisle, for its part, can't be chosen in a “Multi” list.

9.14 Access turned off at first

Access for AI assistants is turned off until the person who runs the server puts LISTO_MCP=on in docker-compose.yml (9.15 Turn on access on the server). While it's off, all its addresses answer as if they didn't exist, and the “AI assistants” section of “My account” doesn't appear.

9.15 Turn on access on the server

For the person who runs the server

Access for AI assistants is turned off by default. To turn it on, add - LISTO_MCP=on in the environment: part of docker-compose.yml, then docker compose up -d. To turn it off: remove the line, then docker compose up -d; all its addresses then answer 404, and the connections already granted stop working (they come back if you turn it on again, as long as they haven't expired). No password or key is needed.

Which assistants: by default, any AI assistant that follows the standard (MCP, with OAuth 2.1 authorization and PKCE) can ask for access, always after the person approves. To accept only some websites, add for example - LISTO_MCP_SITES=claude.ai,claude.com (website names separated by commas, without https:// or a path): only those websites, and apps on the computer (localhost, like Claude Code), can then connect. Connections already granted to another website stop working (without being deleted: they come back if you put the website back). A badly written value keeps Listo from starting, with a message in docker logs listo. Listo only reads an app's identification document (CIMD) on a short list of websites (claude.ai, claude.com, or those in LISTO_MCP_SITES); other assistants register on their own.

Proxy: nothing to set up, these addresses go through the same host. They must stay reachable from the Internet: an online assistant calls them from its servers, an app on the computer (Claude Code) from your computer. Claude as an example: the subdomain must have an IPv4 address (DNS ‘A’ record), because Claude doesn't use IPv6; if a firewall filters incoming connections, don't copy an address range found somewhere else: Anthropic publishes the up-to-date list of addresses these calls come from on its official ‘IP addresses’ page, section ‘Outbound IP addresses’, and may change it. For another assistant, see its documentation.

9.16 Rules and log

For the person who runs the server

  • Access token: 1 hour; refresh token: 30 rolling days, replaced at each use; an old token presented again brings down the whole connection. The database only keeps a fingerprint of the tokens, never the tokens themselves.
  • An AI assistant's token opens nothing but /mcp: not the app, not its other addresses.
  • Return addresses: https on a website name (never an IP address), without a username or fragment, or the computer (localhost); with LISTO_MCP_SITES, only those websites. They are checked at registration and at every authorization. An error is only sent back without a screen to the computer or a known website (claude.ai, claude.com, or those in LISTO_MCP_SITES): otherwise, Listo shows its error page, so it never acts as a relay to another website.
  • Changing your password, or an account that is disabled or whose password is reset (including with the recovery command): all its AI assistant connections are revoked.
  • The caps (9.8 Limits) are counted in memory: they start again from zero when the container restarts.
  • The Admin doesn't see other people's connections. In an emergency, they disable the account or reset its password. For everyone at once: remove LISTO_MCP=on.

Security log (never a token, a code or the content of a list; the names start with claude. for every assistant): claude.connected, claude.refused, claude.revoked, claude.refresh, claude.registered, claude.client_refused, claude.tool (one line per tool call, with the number of items, never a typed name), claude.token_refused, claude.origin_refused.

9.17 First try

For the person who runs the server

After turning it on, once, before any regular use (here with Claude):

  1. In a browser: https://listo.exemple.fr/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp shows a short text with "resource": "https://listo.exemple.fr/mcp".
  2. Connect the assistant (9.9 Connect an AI assistant).
  3. Ask the assistant ‘What are my Listo lists?’, then ‘What's on the shopping list?’: the answers must match the app. With “Read and write”: ‘Add milk to the shopping list’, ‘Check off the bread’; on a phone open on the list, the changes show up live.
  4. docker logs listo: lines claude.connected and claude.tool, no errors. Report any claude.origin_refused or claude.client_refused lines.
  5. More than an hour later, ask another question: it must work without a new authorization.
  6. If there's a problem: remove LISTO_MCP=on, docker compose up -d, and pass on the claude. lines from the log (they contain neither tokens nor list content).

10. FAQ and troubleshooting

10.1 I have no network

Keep going: adding, editing and checking work offline, and the badge at the top counts the pending changes (5.17 Offline and sync). They go out on their own when the network comes back. Sharing, the public link, the trash and reservations wait for the network.

10.2 A photo won't upload

  • With no network, the photo waits for the network to come back.
  • The message “Photo too large.”: the proxy refuses uploads over 2 MB (3.4 What the proxy must do). With nginx or Nginx Proxy Manager, the line client_max_body_size 5m; is missing (3.5 Example with Nginx Proxy Manager).

10.3 A public link doesn't work anymore

It has expired, or its owner stopped it, or the list was deleted (restoring a list doesn't bring its link back), or the note is in the trash. Ask the owner for a new link (8.6 Owner: the public link). If it asks for a password, it's the one the owner wrote down when creating it.

10.4 Changes arrive a minute late

The proxy is holding back the real-time messages (3.4 What the proxy must do). With nginx or Nginx Proxy Manager, the lines proxy_buffering off;, proxy_cache off; and proxy_read_timeout 3600s; are missing (3.5 Example with Nginx Proxy Manager).

10.5 Blank screen or frozen app

  • Close Listo and open it again with a network connection: it picks up the latest version. If the “New version available” banner stays on screen, tap “Reload”.
  • Otherwise: Settings, “My account”, “Reset this phone's data” (on a computer: “Reset this device's data”); pending changes are kept.
  • As a last resort, clear the site's data in the browser (Chrome: padlock to the left of the address, ‘Site settings’, ‘Delete data’; iPhone: Settings, Safari, Advanced, Website Data), then sign in again.
  • Android, installed app, bottom of the screen hidden by the navigation bar: close the app completely, then open it again; otherwise uninstall it and install it again (wait for “Up to date” first, so you don't lose anything).
  • Every phone is affected: the person who runs the server checks docker logs listo and https://listo.exemple.fr/healthz (operations guide, in the repository, in French).

10.6 I can't sign in

  • “Too many attempts”: 5 wrong passwords block signing in for 15 minutes. Wait, or the person who runs the server restarts Listo (docker compose restart).
  • “Request rejected: unknown origin.”: the address you typed isn't exactly the one in LISTO_PUBLIC_URL (another subdomain, http instead of https) (3.2 The folder and docker-compose.yml).
  • Forgotten password, lost phone: 4.8 Forgotten password, lost phone.
  • Offline mode or installing on the home screen doesn't work: Listo isn't opened over HTTPS. Always go through https://listo.exemple.fr, never through http://IP:13300.

10.7 Two-factor authentication: locked out

  • Phone changed or lost: sign in with one of your “Recovery codes” (“Use a recovery code”), then turn two-factor authentication back on with the new phone (4.7 Two-factor authentication and recovery codes).
  • No phone and no recovery codes left: the Admin chooses “Disable two-factor authentication” on your account (“Administration”, “Accounts”, ⋮ button). You sign in with just your password, then turn it back on.
  • It's the Admin who is locked out: see 4.9 Lost Admin account (recovery command with --remove-2fa).
  • The 6-digit code is always refused: the phone's clock may be wrong; set it to automatic.
  • Nobody can get their codes accepted anymore after a server failure: the secret key may have been lost (10.8 The secret key was lost).

10.8 The secret key was lost

For the person who runs the server

Without a copy of secrets/secret.key, Listo creates a new key at startup. Lists, items, photos and passwords aren't affected, but:

  • two-factor authentication can no longer be read: the Admin disables it for the other accounts (“Disable two-factor authentication”), for themselves with the recovery command and --remove-2fa (4.9 Lost Admin account), then everyone turns it back on;
  • public links already created still work, but their address can no longer be shown again: stop them and create new ones;
  • this time, keep a copy of the new key (3.7 Keep the secret key safe).

10.9 Knowing a new version exists

In Settings, the “About” line shows the installed version, or the “Update” badge when a newer version exists. Tap it: on the “About” page, on the version line, the Admin sees the status of the check:

  • “Up to date”: the installed image is the latest one built;
  • ‘Checked 15 min ago’, with a “New version” badge followed by its number (for example ‘New version: 9f8e7d6’): a newer image exists; to get it: operations guide, in the repository, in French;
  • “Couldn't check”: GitHub didn't answer. No consequence: Listo works normally;
  • the refresh button, at the end of the line, asks again right away (once a minute at most).

It's the server (not the phone) that asks GitHub, at most once an hour, with no account or token, which is the latest commit whose image was built. Listo never updates itself. Other accounts only see the version. To turn off the check: LISTO_UPDATE_CHECK=off in docker-compose.yml (the line is already there, commented out), then docker compose up -d.

10.10 A typo keeps coming back in the suggestions

The Admin: Settings, “Administration”, “Shopping” tab, “Items”. Search for the name and “Correct”: Listo says how many items and in how many shopping lists it will be corrected, then corrects it everywhere (if it already exists, the two are merged). “Remove” only takes it out of the suggestions, without touching the lists. A corrected or removed name can come back if someone types it again.

10.11 Wish list: a participant has left

A relative reserved, then left the list or had their account disabled: they remain a participant. On a list created for someone else, the owner sees them and can “Remove” them, or “Release” the whole wish. On a ‘for me’ list, nobody can (the owner is the recipient and doesn't see reservations, that's on purpose): the other participants can only open or close the reservation. If the relative was only disabled, the Admin can turn their account back on long enough for them to “Leave”; otherwise, delete the wish and create it again.
